PART ONE: THE OBSERVATION CYCLE

§1. The Three Operations of One Step

The dynamics of the world consist of a repeated cycle of three fundamental operations:

    (i)   Application of Y in ℂ⁷
    (ii)  Observation: project Re(ψ) onto S⁶ by normalisation
    (iii) Fold: enforce τ ≥ 0 by negating states with τ < 0

These three operations together constitute the "breath" of the system. The laws
discussed in Part Two are additional perturbations layered on top of this fundamental
cycle. Understanding the cycle alone is necessary before understanding the laws.

§2. The Y Application in ℂ⁷

States are maintained as complex vectors in ℂ⁷. The application of Y is exact matrix
multiplication:

    ψ ← Y · ψ

This moves the state off S⁶ (norms are not preserved by Y in general) and into the
full complex space. The imaginary components of ψ accumulate during this step,
encoding the phase rotation associated with each eigenmode. Specifically:

    — The eternal modes (λ = ±1) rotate by ±1 (sign flip for λ = −1, identity for λ = +1).
    — The spiral modes (λ = ±i√3/2) rotate by ±90° and shrink by factor √3/2.
    — The nilpotent modes (λ = 0) are annihilated; they vanish exactly after 3 steps.

After Y, the state is in general a complex vector with nonzero imaginary part. The
observation step will discard the imaginary part in the next operation.

§3. The Observation Step: A Theorem About τ

The observation step takes the real part of the post-Y state and normalises it:

    obs(ψ) = Re(ψ) / ‖Re(ψ)‖

This is a projection from ℂ⁷ back to S⁶ ⊂ ℝ⁷. The imaginary components accumulated
during Y application are discarded. The state is returned to a real unit vector.

The first component of ψ after observation is:

    τ_observed = Re(Y·ψ)[0] / ‖Re(Y·ψ)‖

A key theorem follows directly from the Y matrix structure:

THEOREM (τ-Annihilation). For any REAL state ψ₀ (i.e., a state ψ₀ ∈ ℝ⁷), the τ
component of Y·ψ₀ is determined entirely by the x-component of ψ₀:

    Re[(Y·ψ₀)[0]] = −(1/√2) · ψ₀[x]

Proof. The Y matrix row for τ is:
    (Y·ψ)[0] = −i·ψ[a₁] + (−1/√2 + i/√2)·ψ[x]

For real ψ₀, ψ₀[a₁] and ψ₀[x] are real numbers. Therefore:
    (Y·ψ₀)[0] = −i·ψ₀[a₁] + (−1/√2 + i/√2)·ψ₀[x]
              = −(1/√2)·ψ₀[x]  +  i·(−ψ₀[a₁] + (1/√2)·ψ₀[x])

The real part is −(1/√2)·ψ₀[x] exactly. □

Corollary: If ψ₀[x] = 0 (the x-component is zero), then Re[(Y·ψ₀)[0]] = 0, and
after observation, τ = 0 exactly.

This explains the oscillation observed in the period-2 orbit: a state initialised
with ψ₀[x] = 0 produces τ = 0 after the first Y step and observation. The non-zero
τ at even steps comes from the x-component that Y builds into the state after the
first step.

§4. The Period-2 Orbit

Starting from any real state ψ₀ with τ₀ > 0 and ψ₀[x] = 0 (which includes the
genesis-seeded island states), the trajectory under repeated Y + obs + fold is:

    Step 0: τ = τ₀    (initial high-τ state, x = 0)
    Step 1: τ = 0     (Y annihilates τ because x = 0; weight moves to a₂, b₁)
    Step 2: τ = τ*    (Y has built x-component from the a₂/b₁ weight; τ reconstructed)
    Step 3: τ = 0     (cycle repeats)
    Step 4: τ = τ*    ...

where τ* is determined by the initial conditions (tested numerically: τ₀ = 0.76
gives τ* ≈ 0.56 after settling). The orbit is period-2 in τ after the first step.

The fold ensures both phases have τ > 0 by reflecting the negative-τ representative
of each antipodal pair. Without the fold, the orbit would be τ₀, 0, −τ*, 0, τ*, 0,
−τ*, ... — period-4 in signed τ. With the fold, τ is always non-negative, giving
the observed period-2.

This is why the codebase refers to a "period-4 orbit": it refers to the signed orbit
(two complete high-low-negative_high-zero cycles = 4 Y applications) before the fold
symmetry is applied. The operative period in the folded simulation is 2.

§5. The High-τ Phase and the Double Step

The simulation settles cells onto the high-τ phase of the orbit. This is achieved by
the "double step" in law_double_step: two Y applications are made before each
observation, so the effective step always lands on the high-τ phase rather than
alternating.

Specifically: starting from the high-τ phase (τ = τ*), a single Y step gives τ = 0;
a second Y step gives Re(τ) ≠ 0 again (because Y has built up x-component in the
state). The double step thus guarantees that after each combined operation
(Y² + obs + fold), cells return to the high-τ phase. The τ value seen at each step
is always the "settled" value, not the transient zero.

This is a deliberate choice: it keeps material classifications stable from step to
step (otherwise cells would alternate between stone and water at every step, which
would be physically meaningless). The double step is the mechanism for making
material classification temporally coherent.

PART TWO: THE SEVEN LAWS

§6. Taxonomy of the Laws

The complete step function applies seven laws to every active cell at each time step,
in the following order:

    1. Living-Y field: compute spatially-varying Y application strength α(i)
    2. Double step: Y² applied with strength α, then obs + fold
    3. Coherence-gated diffusion: local state exchange weighted by coherence × proximity
    4. Sea-level protection: restore τ for cells in the ocean basin
    5. Pole pull: Riemannian gradient step toward the dominant material pole
    6. Genesis restore: altitude-based τ correction
    7. Observation + fold: final normalisation after all perturbations

Each law perturbs the state on S⁶. Laws 3–6 perturb off S⁶ (by adding vectors to
normalised states), and law 7 returns everything to S⁶. The perturbations are all
small relative to the norm of the state; they are corrections on top of the
fundamental Y dynamics, not competitors to them.

§7. Law 1: The Living-Y Field

The Y-application strength is not uniform across the island. Each cell i is assigned:

    α(i) = ALPHA_Y × (mean-τ of local neighbourhood − mean-τ of whole island)

where the neighbourhood is defined by the same Gaussian kernel as the diffusion law.
Stone cells (high local τ) receive positive α: Y is applied more strongly, making
their period-2 orbit faster and their τ values more extreme (higher at high phase,
lower at low phase). Water cells receive negative α: Y is applied less strongly,
producing slower oscillation and reduced τ contrast.

The parameter ALPHA_Y = 0.02 is a small perturbation: it modulates Y application
strength by ±2% per unit of τ deviation from the mean. This is small enough that
the fundamental period-2 orbit is preserved everywhere, but large enough to create
measurable spatial variation in the effective oscillation frequency.

The physical consequence: a spatial gradient in effective Y-strength exists across
the island, from the high-α stone highlands to the low-α water basin. The
zero-crossing of α (where stone and water have equal breathing rate) marks the
dynamic coastline — the locus where exchange between materials is most active.

ALPHA_Y = 0.02 is a calibrated parameter. Its role is to create a small spatial
gradient; its precise value determines the living-Y beat period (~395 steps) but
does not qualitatively change the material structure.

§8. Law 2: The Double Step

The core dynamic law:

    S ← Y² · S  (modulated by α)
    S ← obs(fold(S))

The Y² application is the double-step that keeps cells in the high-τ phase as derived
in §5. The α modulation makes the effective Y-application spatially varying; in
practice this is implemented by interpolating between Y and Y² based on α:

    S ← α·(Y²·S) + (1−α)·S  approximately, then normalise

This captures the idea that strong-α cells apply Y more completely (closer to
Y²), while weak-α cells retain more of their current state.

§9. Law 3: Coherence-Gated Diffusion

The diffusion law mixes each cell's state with its neighbours, weighted by a combined
spatial-spectral gate:

    W[i,j] ∝ coh(sᵢ, sⱼ) × exp(−|pᵢ−pⱼ|² / 2σ²)    if coh > θ
           = 0                                           otherwise

where coh(s₁, s₂) = |⟨s₁|s₂⟩|² / (‖s₁‖²‖s₂‖²), σ = LOC_SIGMA = 1.0 (in genesis
coordinate units), and θ = COH_THRESHOLD = 0.10.

The updated state for cell i is:

    sᵢ ← (1−t)·sᵢ + t · Σⱼ W[i,j]·sⱼ   (t = t_diffuse = 0.06)

The parameter t_diffuse = 0.06 means each cell retains 94% of its current state
and takes 6% from the neighbourhood average.

AN IMPORTANT CLARIFICATION ON COH_THRESHOLD. At convergence, measured coherence values
between material classes are all substantially above 0.10:

    Stone-stone (same material): mean coherence ≈ 0.92
    Sand-sand (same material):   mean coherence ≈ 0.97
    Water-water (same material): mean coherence ≈ 1.00
    Stone-water (cross):         mean coherence ≈ 0.60
    Stone-sand  (cross):         mean coherence ≈ 0.85
    Sand-water  (cross):         mean coherence ≈ 0.89

Every cross-material coherence exceeds 0.10 by a large margin. COH_THRESHOLD does
NOT prevent stone-water diffusion at convergence; it would only prevent diffusion
between states with ≥ 71.6° angular separation.

The primary mechanism preventing materials from mixing is therefore SPATIAL LOCALITY,
not coherence gating. Stone cells are in the highlands; water cells are in the ocean
basin. The Gaussian kernel exp(−d²/2σ²) with σ = 1.0 ensures that only cells within
a few genesis-coordinate units exchange state. Because stone and water are spatially
separated by the topographic gradient, they rarely have the proximity to diffuse.

The role of COH_THRESHOLD = 0.10 is different from what might be expected. It gates
diffusion between TRANSIENT states and settled states: at seed, air cells (near-nil
states) have coherence ~0.28 with stone cells — well above 0.10 — but the spatial
separation still prevents significant exchange. The threshold matters most in the
early settling period (steps 0–5) and in pathological cases where very unusual states
are born at the boundary. At convergence it is effectively inert.

This is not a design flaw: the system is correctly stabilised by spatial structure,
not by spectral gating. The spectral gate would be important in a system without
spatial extent (a single-cell world), where it would prevent the nilpotent transient
from polluting the settled dynamics.

§10. Law 4: Sea-Level Protection

The sea-level law is a boundary condition for the ocean basin. Cells below a
threshold altitude (determined by their genesis position) that have drifted toward
sand (τ approaching TAU_SAND from below) receive a small restorative pull back toward
lower τ:

    if altitude < sea_level AND τ > τ_water_upper_bound:
        sᵢ ← sᵢ − ε · (τᵢ − τ_target) · ê_τ    (approximately)

This prevents the ocean from drying out: without this law, the diffusion and pole-pull
laws would gradually drag all cells toward stone over very long timescales (the stone
pole is a stronger attractor than the water pole due to the asymmetric driving from
genesis-restore). The sea-level law enforces an asymmetric boundary condition: the
ocean basin has a lower τ ceiling than the rest of the island.

Law 4 is the only law with explicit reference to external geometry (altitude). All
other laws operate entirely in spectral space.

§11. Law 5: Pole Pull

The pole-pull law implements a Riemannian gradient ascent on the function
f(s) = coh(s, w_dominant), where w_dominant is the material pole (stone or water)
that the cell has greater coherence with.

For a cell with state s (normalised), the gradient of f(s) = |⟨s, w⟩|² on S⁶ is
the component of w perpendicular to s:

    ∇_{S⁶} f(s) = w⊥   where  w⊥ = w − ⟨w, s⟩·s

The update step is:

    s ← normalise(s + ε · w⊥)

where ε = POLE_PULL × dominant_coh × |bias| / mean_coh. The step size ε is small
(POLE_PULL = 0.10 is the maximum, reduced further by the bias and coherence factors).

EQUATORIAL CELLS. The bias term |coh₁ − coh₂| measures how strongly a cell prefers
one pole over the other. For cells with coh₁ ≈ coh₂ (equal coherence with both poles),
the bias is near zero and ε ≈ 0: these cells feel effectively no net pull. They
remain in the equatorial locus — the saddle zone between the two attractors.

This equatorial locus is the geometrically distinguished region of the island: it is
the locus of cells that are equidistant (in spectral coherence) from the stone and
water poles. Sand cells, which have intermediate τ values, are precisely the cells
that tend to sit near this equatorial locus. The sand zone is therefore not just a
material classification but a geometric feature of the two-pole attractor structure:
it is the nodal surface of the pull field.

§12. Law 6: Genesis Restore

Every cell has a "home τ" determined at birth by its genesis altitude z:

    τ_home(i) = TAU_WATER + (TAU_STONE − TAU_WATER) × z_norm(i)

where z_norm is the normalised altitude (0 = lowest, 1 = highest point of the island).
This is a linear interpolation: highland cells have high τ_home (near TAU_STONE);
lowland cells have low τ_home (near TAU_WATER).

The law: cells with τ < τ_home receive a corrective pull toward the stone pole
(which drives τ upward), with strength proportional to the τ deficit:

    pressure = τ_home − τ_current
    if pressure > RESTORE_MIN_PRESS = 0.05:
        β = clip(RESTORE_STRENGTH × pressure / (TAU_STONE − TAU_WATER), 0, 0.92)
        s ← (1 − β) · s + β · w_stone

The term β is the blend coefficient: it interpolates the cell toward the stone pole
proportionally to how far below its home τ it has drifted.

MATHEMATICAL STRUCTURE. Genesis restore is the Free Energy Principle in explicit
algebraic form. The cell's genesis altitude encodes a prior: "a cell born at this
altitude ought to have approximately this τ." Deviations from the prior generate a
corrective force proportional to the deviation. The strength parameter RESTORE_STRENGTH
= 0.80 is calibrated to make the restoration fast but not instantaneous — strong enough
to maintain geological stratification, gentle enough to allow natural fluctuation.

FIXED-POINT PROPERTY. After genesis restore has operated for a few steps, cells that
have settled near their τ_home no longer receive corrections (pressure < 0.05). The
law becomes inert for well-settled cells and active only for cells that have drifted
significantly from their geological prior. This creates a stable equilibrium: cells
far from home are corrected; cells at home are left alone.

§13. Law 7: Final Observation + Fold

After all perturbative laws have been applied, the states are off S⁶ (their norms
are no longer 1). The final law returns them:

    S ← obs(fold(S)) = Re(S) / ‖Re(S)‖,  with τ ← |τ|

This is identical to the observation step in the fundamental cycle (§3). It discards
accumulated imaginary components and returns all states to the unit sphere.

The order matters: genesis restore, pole pull, and sea-level laws modify the state
in ℂ⁷; the final obs+fold collapses all these modifications simultaneously into a
single normalised real vector. This is the "measurement" step: the complex evolution
during the step is projected onto the observable (real, normalised) state.

PART THREE: THE TIMESCALE HIERARCHY

§14. Nested Timescales

The Y₃₂₃ dynamical system contains a natural hierarchy of timescales. From fastest
to slowest:

    3 steps:        Nilpotent cascade termination
                    The λ = 0 eigenspace is annihilated exactly within 3 Y applications.
                    Any transient component in this space vanishes in finite, bounded time.
                    This is the fastest meaningful dynamical event.

    2 steps:        Period-2 τ orbit
                    Each cell's τ alternates between high-τ and zero every step (fold
                    makes it high-τ then low-positive-τ). The double-step law ensures
                    the observable state is always at the high-τ phase.

    ~4.82 steps:    Spiral mode half-life
                    Energy in the ±i√3/2 eigenspace decays by a factor of (√3/2)² = 3/4
                    per step in energy (factor √3/2 in amplitude). Half-life is
                    log(2)/log(2/√3) ≈ 4.82 steps. The spiral modes carry the system's
                    "working memory" — information relevant for the next ~5 steps.

    ~30 steps:      Material stratification settling time
                    The transition from seed (mostly air) to settled island (stone,
                    sand, water) takes approximately 5–30 steps. The convergence scalar
                    Ψ rises from ~0.003 to ~0.65–0.70 in the first 5 steps as genesis
                    restore eliminates air. Full stratification to Ψ > 0.85 takes ~60
                    steps.

    ~100 steps:     Material boundary migration
                    The locations of stone/sand/water boundaries shift over roughly
                    100-step timescales as emergence adds new cells at the coastline.

    ~395 steps:     Living-Y beat period
                    The interference between spatially-varying Y-application rates
                    across the island creates a quasi-periodic modulation of the mean
                    τ distribution. The period is ~395 steps and is an emergent property
                    of ALPHA_Y, the island extent, and the fundamental period-2 orbit.

    ~1000 steps:    Pole polarisation epoch
                    At POLE_PULL = 0.10, the two-pole attractor takes approximately
                    1000 steps to fully sort the island into two distinct hemispheres
                    (stone-biased and water-biased). Before this timescale, pole
                    separation is present but soft; after it, the two hemispheres are
                    sharply defined.

    Permanent:      Genesis altitude memory
                    Genesis restore encodes altitude as a permanent prior. Cells that
                    are at their τ_home remain there indefinitely. The geological
                    stratification is self-maintaining once established.

    ∞:              Eternal mode persistence
                    Energy in the λ = ±1 eigenspace is preserved exactly. This
                    timescale is genuinely infinite: eternal modes never decay.

§15. How the Hierarchy Arises

The fast timescales (3 steps, 2 steps, ~5 steps) are direct consequences of the Y
eigenspectrum. They are mathematical facts, derivable without running the simulation.

The medium timescales (~30 steps, ~100 steps) arise from the spatial structure of
the island interacting with the fast dynamics. They require the specific geometry to
predict but are qualitatively predictable.

The slow timescale (~395 steps) arises from the competition between two nearly-
commensurable frequencies: the period-2 τ orbit and the ALPHA_Y-induced spatial
modulation. The beat frequency is their difference.

The very slow timescale (~1000 steps) arises from the accumulation of many small
geodesic steps under pole-pull. The total angular displacement of a cell from its
initial position to its final pole-dominated position is ~15° over ~1000 steps at
POLE_PULL = 0.10 × typical coherence factor.

This hierarchy — from 2 steps to effectively infinite — spans seven orders of
magnitude in timescale, all arising from a single 7×7 matrix and three additional
calibrated parameters (ALPHA_Y, POLE_PULL, RESTORE_STRENGTH). The hierarchy is
not programmed in; it emerges from the interaction of the eigenstructure with the
spatial geometry.

PART FOUR: BIRTH AND DEATH

§16. The Gram Matrix Birth Mechanism

New cells are born from the boundary — the interface between materials — via the
following mechanism.

At each step, the Gram matrix of the boundary population is computed:

    G = Sₙᵀ · Sₙ    (7×7 complex matrix)

where Sₙ are the normalised states of boundary cells, weighted by their boundary
score (a measure of how much they sit at a material interface rather than in a
homogeneous region). The eigenvectors of G with largest eigenvalues are the
principal collective modes of the boundary — the configurations that best represent
the coherent structure of the current frontier.

New cells are born as these eigenvectors: the top eigenmode of G becomes the state
of the first new cell, the second eigenmode becomes the second new cell, and so on
up to max_births = 6 per step.

Measured values of the Gram eigenvalues at step 30:

    λ₁ = 334.06  (dominant mode, |τ| component ≈ 0.58)
    λ₂ =  42.99  (secondary mode, |τ| component ≈ 0.59)
    λ₃ =   1.78  (tertiary mode,  |τ| component ≈ 0.56)
    λ₄ < 0.17   (noise modes, negligible contribution)

The large gap between λ₁ and λ₃ (334 vs 1.78) shows that the boundary is
highly coherent: it is dominated by a single collective mode with a handful of
secondary variations. New cells born at the boundary are nearly identical to the
top Gram eigenmode.

The τ components of all three significant modes are ~0.58, which places them
squarely in the sand regime (0.40 < τ < 0.65). The boundary births sand cells.
This is not coincidental: the sand zone IS the boundary, and the Gram matrix is
sampling that boundary, so its eigenvectors naturally have sand-like τ values.

§17. The Birth Position

The position of a newly born cell is the centroid of its "parents" — the top-K
boundary cells weighted by their contribution to the Gram eigenmode and their
boundary score. With K = 3, the birth position is the average of the three cells
that most strongly contribute to the eigenmode:

    p_new = Σᵢ wᵢ · pᵢ / Σᵢ wᵢ    where wᵢ = score[i] for top-K parents

This places new cells spatially inside the boundary region. They are born at the
coastline, not in the interior highlands or the deep ocean.

§18. Cell Death

A cell is culled (removed from the active population) when its state norm falls
below DEATH_NORM = 10⁻⁵:

    cull: active[i] ← (‖ψᵢ‖ > 10⁻⁵)

This threshold is far below the typical active-cell norm (1.0 after observation).
Culling primarily removes cells that have had their state collapsed by the genesis
restore law (fully blended toward w_stone with β = 0.92 gives a norm of 0.92 ×
‖w_stone‖ ≈ 0.92, still well above the death threshold). In practice, culling
removes cells that have undergone many near-nil perturbations and accumulated
near-zero state through pathological interactions. It is a housekeeping mechanism
rather than a primary demographic force.

The population is approximately stable: births (up to 6 per step from Gram
emergence) balance deaths over long runs. The island grows slowly from its initial
1287 cells to approximately 1500 by step 80, at which point the growth rate
decreases as the boundary reaches a mature, stable configuration.

PART FIVE: THE CONVERGENCE THEORY

§19. Ψ as a Lyapunov Function

The convergence scalar Ψ ∈ [0, 1] was defined in Paper One. Here we examine its
dynamical behaviour as evidence for the Lyapunov structure of the system.

A Lyapunov function for a dynamical system is a scalar that is:
    (i)  bounded above (by 1)
    (ii) non-decreasing along trajectories in the absence of perturbation
    (iii) maximised at the attractor
    (iv) positive definite relative to the attractor

Ψ satisfies (i) and (iii) by construction. Properties (ii) and (iv) are empirical
claims subject to verification.

MEASURED TRAJECTORY. Across all tested initial conditions (four independent runs):

    Seed (step 0):    Ψ ≈ 0.003 (nearly zero: mostly air, undifferentiated)
    Step 1:           Ψ ≈ 0.666 (genesis restore eliminates most air immediately)
    Step 5:           Ψ ≈ 0.652 (slight dip: solid fraction temporarily too high)
    Step 15–25:       Ψ ≈ 0.648–0.732 (slow ascent: material boundaries settling)
    Step 30–55:       Ψ ≈ 0.738–0.768 (plateau: stratification near equilibrium)
    Step 56–75:       Ψ ≈ 0.768–0.930 (rapid ascent: solid fraction approaching target)
    Step 75+:         Ψ → 0.941 (asymptotic approach to attractor)

CRITICAL OBSERVATION. The trajectory is NOT monotone. There is a dip at steps 5–15
and a plateau at steps 30–55. Ψ is therefore NOT a Lyapunov function in the strict
sense.

This is correct and expected: a strict Lyapunov function would require the system
to have no local oscillations or temporary regressions. The Y₃₂₃ world has both:
    — The period-2 τ orbit creates microscopic oscillations
    — The living-Y beat period (~395 steps) creates macroscopic modulations
    — Birth events can temporarily reduce solid fraction (Ψ_S)

Ψ is better understood as a QUASI-LYAPUNOV FUNCTION: it is monotone in the long run
(trend increasing) while exhibiting transient fluctuations. More precisely:

    Ψ(t) is non-decreasing in the MOVING AVERAGE over PSI_WINDOW = 20 steps

The convergence criterion dΨ/dt < PSI_CONVERGE_THR = 2×10⁻⁴ (evaluated over the
20-step moving average) detects the end of meaningful dynamics: when the world's
moving average of Ψ has stabilised, the transient oscillations are too small to
distinguish from converged behaviour.

§20. The Unique Attractor

The most striking empirical fact about the Y₃₂₃ system is the uniqueness of its
attractor. Across all tested seeds (four independent runs), the convergence value is:

    Ψ(step 80) = 0.9411 ± 0.000000

The zero standard deviation is not a rounding artifact: the trajectory is identical
across all seeds to at least four decimal places. This means:

    (i)  The system has a unique attractor (up to the symmetries of the seed)
    (ii) The genesis island geometry fully determines the final state
    (iii) There are no other competing attractors at POLE_PULL = 0.10 and the tested parameters

This is a non-trivial result. The system has many degrees of freedom (1400+ cells ×
7 dimensions each), many nonlinear laws, and complex boundary dynamics. That it
converges to the same state from all initial conditions suggests a strong contraction
property: the attractor's basin of attraction is effectively the entire initial
condition space (for this island geometry).

§21. The Phase Diagram of Ψ

The Ψ trajectory can be understood in terms of which component is limiting at each
phase:

Phase 1 (steps 0–1): Ψ_KL is near zero, dragging Ψ to near zero.
    The KL divergence from the target distribution is large because the initial
    island has ~28% air cells (τ < 0.15), and the target has 0% air.
    Genesis restore fires aggressively and eliminates air within ~5 steps.
    Ψ_KL jumps from ~0 to ~0.95 in the first step.

Phase 2 (steps 1–30): Ψ_S is limiting.
    The solid fraction f_solid starts around 0.48 (too high vs target 0.34).
    The island has too many stone+sand cells relative to water.
    Ψ_S ≈ 0.43–0.57 during this phase, below the Ψ_F ≈ 0.65–0.74 and Ψ_KL ≈ 0.95.
    The system is "too solid" — new water cells need to be born to bring the
    solid fraction toward the target.

Phase 3 (steps 30–80): Ψ_S rises as the island grows.
    New cells are born via emergence (primarily at the sand/water boundary).
    The newly born cells have τ ≈ 0.58 (sand), which only slightly increases solid
    fraction. But as the ocean fills in with new cells, the overall water fraction
    grows. f_solid drops from 0.48 to ~0.35, driving Ψ_S from 0.43 to 0.85.

Phase 4 (step 80+): Convergence.
    All three components are near their maximum simultaneously.
    Ψ_F ≈ 0.71 (class separation stable), Ψ_S ≈ 0.85 (solid fraction near target),
    Ψ_KL ≈ 0.95 (distribution near target). Ψ ≈ 0.94.

§22. The Geometric Meaning of the Attractor

The attractor Ψ ≈ 0.94 corresponds to a world where:

    — Three material classes are sharply separated in τ space (Ψ_F ≈ 0.71)
    — ~35% of cells are solid (stone + sand), ~65% are water (Ψ_S ≈ 0.85)
    — The distribution closely matches [12%, 22%, 66%, 0%] (Ψ_KL ≈ 0.95)
    — The stone and water poles are separated by ~31.5° on S⁶
    — The pole separation angle has zero variance across seeds (unique attractor)

In spectral terms: at the attractor, the stone pole w_stone and water pole w_water
represent the two dominant collective modes of the island population. The island
has organised itself into two spectral hemispheres with a sand equatorial band.
The convergence is geometric: the two poles have settled into the specific
configuration that Y produces when acting on the natural geological distribution.

PART SIX: THE COHERENCE STRUCTURE

§23. What Coherence Measures Here

The coherence metric |⟨s₁|s₂⟩|² / (‖s₁‖²‖s₂‖²) in this context is not a quantum
mechanical fidelity in the physical sense. It is a geometric quantity: the squared
cosine of the angle between two vectors on S⁶. It ranges from 0 (perpendicular =
no relationship) to 1 (parallel = identical state).

What the measured coherence values reveal about the converged world:

Water-water coherence ≈ 1.00: all water cells have nearly identical states.
The ocean is a homogeneous spectral environment. This makes sense: the ocean basin
is deep, undisturbed by the stone highlands' stiffness, and diffuses freely within
itself. All ocean cells converge to the same τ ≈ 0.29 and the same direction on S⁶.

Sand-sand coherence ≈ 0.97: sand cells are highly coherent with each other.
The coastline has a characteristic spectral signature that is nearly uniform along
its length, despite being a spatially extended region.

Stone-stone coherence ≈ 0.92: stone cells show slightly more variation.
The highlands are topographically varied (different altitudes, different α values),
creating more spectral diversity within the stone material than within the flat ocean.

Stone-water coherence ≈ 0.60: large but not maximal. Stone and water are
substantially coherent across materials. They share the same Y dynamics and the same
manifold, so they are not orthogonal. The 0.60 coherence means they are
approximately 39° apart on S⁶ in this direction (arccos(√0.60) ≈ 39.2°), which is
distinct from the 31.5° pole separation because coherence is measured between
individual cells, not between the pole vectors.

§24. The Diffusion Network

The coherence-gated diffusion law creates an effective network of information exchange
on the island. At convergence, this network has the following structure:

    WITHIN water: very high weight (coherence ≈ 1.00 × proximity)
                  The ocean is a single connected component with nearly unrestricted
                  diffusion. Ocean cells form a spectral community.

    WITHIN stone: high weight (coherence ≈ 0.92 × proximity)
                  The highlands form a nearly connected component. Stone cells
                  effectively share state with all nearby stone cells.

    ACROSS stone/water: moderate weight (coherence ≈ 0.60 × proximity)
                  Prevented primarily by spatial separation. The shoreline is
                  narrow, so very few stone cells are close to water cells.

    ACROSS sand/water: moderate weight (coherence ≈ 0.89 × proximity)
                  Sand cells on the water side of the coastline exchange freely
                  with adjacent water cells. This is the primary source of sand
                  state variation along the coast.

    ACROSS stone/sand: high weight (coherence ≈ 0.85 × proximity)
                  Stone cells near the coastline exchange with adjacent sand cells.
                  This is why the stone/sand boundary is gradual rather than sharp:
                  diffusion partially mixes adjacent stone and sand cell states.

The diffusion network is therefore approximately three connected components (stone,
sand, water) with weak coupling at the boundaries. This explains both the sharpness
of material classification and the existence of graded transitions at the interfaces.

APPENDIX TO PAPER TWO: THE STEP FUNCTION IN FULL

For reference, the complete step function is:

    INPUT:  world (PureWorld with N active cells)
    
    1. Extract active states S (N×7 complex) and positions P (N×3)
    
    2. Compute α = compute_local_Y_alpha(S, P)
       α[i] = ALPHA_Y × (mean τ in σ-neighbourhood of i − global mean τ)
    
    3. S ← law_double_step(S, α)
       S ← Y²·S (α-weighted), then obs(fold(S))
    
    4. S ← law_coherence_diffuse(S, P, t=0.06)
       S ← (1−0.06)·S + 0.06·(W·S)  where W[i,j] ∝ coh(i,j)·exp(−d²/2)
    
    5. S ← law_sea_level(S, P)
       Restore τ for low-altitude cells drifting above τ_water_upper
    
    6. S ← law_pole_pull(S, w_stone, w_water)
       s_i ← normalise(s_i + ε_i · w⊥_dominant(i))
       ε_i = POLE_PULL · max(coh₁ᵢ, coh₂ᵢ) · |coh₁ᵢ − coh₂ᵢ| / mean_coh
    
    7. S ← law_genesis_restore(S, P, w_stone, P_all)
       For cells with τ_i < τ_home(altitude(i)) − 0.05:
       β = clip(0.80 × (τ_home − τ_i) / (TAU_STONE − TAU_WATER), 0, 0.92)
       s_i ← (1−β)·s_i + β·w_stone
    
    8. S ← obs(fold(S))    [final normalisation]
    
    9. new_S, scores ← law_emerge(S, P, threshold)
       Compute Gram G, take top eigenmodes as new cell states
       Place up to max_births=6 new cells at parent centroids
    
    10. Cull: deactivate cells with ‖s_i‖ < 10⁻⁵
    
    11. Compute Ψ = compute_psi(world), append to world.psi_history
    
    12. world.step += 1
    
    OUTPUT: updated world

CORRECTIONS TO PAPER ONE

§ Correction 1: The Role of COH_THRESHOLD

Paper One described COH_THRESHOLD = 0.10 as preventing cross-material diffusion by
sitting "in the gap between intra- and cross-material coherence values." This
characterisation is incorrect. Measured cross-material coherences at convergence are
0.60 (stone-water), 0.85 (stone-sand), and 0.89 (sand-water) — all far above 0.10.
The threshold does not gate cross-material diffusion in the settled world.

The correct characterisation: COH_THRESHOLD prevents diffusion between states that
are more than ~71.6° apart in angle on S⁶. This occurs primarily in the early
settling phase (first ~5 steps) between transient states and settled states, not
between material classes at convergence. The primary mechanism preventing material
mixing at convergence is spatial locality (the Gaussian kernel with σ = 1.0).

§ Correction 2: The Period-4 Claim

Paper One stated the period-4 orbit as given in the codebase without derivation. The
correct analysis (Paper Two §4): the operative orbit under the folded simulation is
period-2 in τ. The period-4 description refers to the unfold orbit in signed τ
(where τ and −τ are tracked separately). Since the fold is always applied in the
simulation, the effective period is 2.
