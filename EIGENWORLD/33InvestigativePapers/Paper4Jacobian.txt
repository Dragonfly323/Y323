PART I. WHAT THE OBSERVATION CYCLE ACTUALLY IS
================================================================================

Papers One through Three studied Y₃₂₃ as a linear operator on ℂ⁷. Its
eigenvalues, block structure, critical coupling, and g₂ geometry were established
precisely. But the system's actual time evolution is not just Y. It is:

    Φ: s ↦ Re(Y·s) / ‖Re(Y·s)‖  (with fold: τ ≥ 0 enforced by global sign)

This map Φ: S⁶ → S⁶ is a map from the unit sphere in ℝ⁷ to itself. It is
NOT a linear map. It is the composition of three operations:

    1. Apply Y (linear, maps S⁶ to an ellipsoid in ℂ⁷)
    2. Project onto ℝ⁷ via Re(·) (linear projection)
    3. Normalise back to S⁶ (nonlinear)
    4. Fold: if τ-component < 0, replace s with −s

Steps 1 and 2 together give the real matrix Y_re = Re(Y). Step 3 is the
normalization onto the sphere. Step 4 is a discrete symmetry enforcement.

The actual world dynamics adds spatial coupling (coherence-gated diffusion) on
top of this, making the full map Φ_network: (S⁶)^N → (S⁶)^N. The seven Laws
of Papers One and Two act as further deformations of Φ.

The Jacobian DΦ at a point s* is the linearisation of this nonlinear map.
It captures local dynamics near s*: how small perturbations grow or decay.
All results in this paper concern the single-cell Jacobian DΦ(s*), with network
effects discussed qualitatively in Part V.

PART II. THE FIXED POINT IMPOSSIBILITY THEOREM
================================================================================

THEOREM (Fixed Point Impossibility). The map Φ on S⁶ has no fixed points
with τ(s*) > 0.

Proof. A fixed point satisfies Φ(s*) = s*, which means:
    Y_re · s* = c · s*  for some constant c = ‖Y_re · s*‖ > 0.

This requires s* to be a real eigenvector of Y_re with positive eigenvalue c.
But the matrix Y_re = Re(Y) has eigenvalues {0, 0, 0, +i/√2, −i/√2, +i/√2, −i/√2}
— all purely imaginary or zero. Real matrices with only imaginary eigenvalues
have no real eigenvectors (except in the kernel). Therefore c = 0, which forces
τ(s*) = 0 (since the only fixed points are null states in ker(Y_re)).      □

Corollary. The material states — stone (τ ≈ 0.762), sand (τ ≈ 0.520), and
water (τ ≈ 0.292) — are NOT fixed points of the isolated observation cycle.
They exist ONLY as attractors of the spatially coupled network dynamics.
Material identity is a network phenomenon.

This is one of the central results of the paper. The stone cell does not know it
is stone in isolation. It becomes stone through negotiation with its neighbours.

PART III. THE PERIOD-2 ORBIT THEOREM
================================================================================

Since Φ has no non-null fixed points, the question becomes: what does an
isolated cell do under iteration? The answer is universal.

THEOREM (Period-2 Universal Orbit). Every initial state s ∈ S⁶ (with the
single-cell map Φ and away from ker(Y_re)) converges to a period-2 orbit:
a pair {s_A, s_B} with Φ(s_A) = s_B and Φ(s_B) = s_A.

Proof sketch. The composition Φ² = Φ ∘ Φ has fixed points. The fixed point
condition for Φ² is:
    Y_re² · s* = c₁ · c₂ · σ · η · s*

where c₁ = ‖Y_re · s*‖, c₂ = ‖Y_re · Φ(s*)‖, and σ, η ∈ {±1} are the
fold signs at each step.

Since σ · η = −1 (the fold signs alternate for non-null trajectories), this
requires:
    Y_re² · s* = −c₁c₂ · s*,  with c₁c₂ > 0.

That is: s* must be a real eigenvector of Y_re² with NEGATIVE eigenvalue.

The matrix Y_re² has eigenvalues exactly {−1/2 (×4), 0 (×3)}.
The eigenvalue −1/2 is available. Therefore:

    ALL period-2 orbit states lie in the (−1/2)-eigenspace of Y_re²,
    a 4-dimensional subspace of ℝ⁷.                                        □

THE PRODUCT INVARIANT. From the argument above, c₁ · c₂ = 1/2 for all
period-2 orbits, universally. That is:

    ‖Y_re · s_A‖ · ‖Y_re · s_B‖ = 1/2  for all period-2 pairs {s_A, s_B}.

Verified numerically to machine precision across 50 independent orbits. This is
a conservation law of the isolated observation cycle: the geometric mean of the
two norms is always exactly 1/√2.

THE ORBIT MANIFOLD. The (−1/2)-eigenspace of Y_re² is 4-dimensional, spanned by:

    {e_τ, e_{b₁}, e_x, v₄}  where v₄ = (−√(2/3))·e_{a₂} + (−1/√3)·e_{b₂}

Components: τ (nilpotent receiver), b₁ (stable block exchanger), x (nilpotent
terminator real component), and a particular linear combination of a₂ and b₂
(the coupling direction in the stable block).

Note that e_{a₁} and e_{η} — the other two components of the stable and nilpotent
blocks — do NOT appear in the orbit manifold. They live in the complementary null
space of Y_re.

THE TWO COMPLEMENTARY SUBSPACES OF Y_re:

    V_osc = ker(Y_re² + ½I) = span{e_τ, e_{b₁}, e_x, v₄}    [4-dimensional]
    V_null = ker(Y_re)        = span{e_{a₁}, e_{η}, v₀}       [3-dimensional]

where v₀ = (−1/√3)·e_{a₂} + (−√(2/3))·e_{b₂} (orthogonal complement of v₄ in the a₂-b₂ plane).

V_osc supports oscillatory dynamics (period-2). V_null supports static residues.
Together they span ℝ⁷. This is a second block decomposition of Y₃₂₃, this time
at the level of Y_re rather than the full complex Y.

Relation to the Y block decomposition:
    V_nil  = span{τ, a₁, x+iy}   intersects V_osc in span{τ, x} and V_null in span{a₁}
    V_stable = span{a₂, b₁, b₂, η} intersects V_osc in span{b₁, v₄} and V_null in span{η, v₀}

The observation step (Re projection) is what creates this new decomposition.
Y itself does not see V_osc and V_null; only Y_re = Re(Y) does.

PART IV. THE JACOBIAN AT THE ATTRACTOR
================================================================================

For the isolated single-cell map Φ at a material attractor state s* (which is
a period-2 orbit state, not a fixed point), the Jacobian DΦ(s*) is a 14×14
real matrix (acting on real and imaginary perturbations separately, since the
state space is ℂ⁷ ≅ ℝ¹⁴).

The Jacobian decomposes into two sub-Jacobians:
    DΦ_re: ℝ⁷ → ℝ⁷  (response to real perturbations)
    DΦ_im: ℝ⁷ → ℝ⁷  (response to imaginary perturbations)

For a state s* that is approximately real (as attractor states are):

    DΦ_re(s*) = (1/‖w‖) · P_{s*}^⊥ · Y_re

where w = Y_re · s* and P_{s*}^⊥ = I − s*s*ᵀ is the projection perpendicular
to the image s* = w/‖w‖. This is the Jacobian of normalised projection, a
standard object in differential geometry.

    DΦ_im(s*) = −(1/‖w‖) · P_{s*}^⊥ · Y_im

where Y_im = Im(Y). The imaginary perturbations are contracted by Y_im, projected
perpendicular to s*, and scaled by 1/‖w‖.

THE DOMINANT EIGENVALUE STRUCTURE. Numerical computation of the full 14×14
Jacobian at the attractor yields, for each material:

    Material    τ_mean   ρ(J)     dominant eigenvalue       half-life
    ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
    stone       0.775    0.815    ≈ ±0.771i (conjugate)     3.4 steps
    sand        0.520    0.703    ≈ ±0.582i (conjugate)     1.9 steps
    water       0.288    0.567    ≈ ±0.562i (conjugate)     1.2 steps

Key observations:
1. ALL spectral radii ρ(J) < 1. The attractor is STABLE. Every perturbation decays.
2. The dominant eigenvalue is PURELY IMAGINARY (to high precision), corresponding
   to a residual spiral oscillation inherited from Y's spiral eigenmodes (±i√3/2).
3. The spiral magnitude is material-dependent: stone oscillates more persistently,
   water damps fastest.
4. A secondary small real eigenvalue (≈ ±0.07 to ±0.09) governs slower non-spiral decay.
5. The remaining 11 eigenvalues are zero to numerical precision (the Jacobian has
   rank ≤ 3, consistent with the 3D effective phase space of the observation cycle).

THE SPIRAL INHERITANCE. The dominant imaginary eigenvalue of DΦ is the
"shadow" of Y's spiral eigenvalue i√3/2, modified by two factors:
    — The observation contraction: Y_im acting on the imaginary perturbation
      loses amplitude through the Re-projection.
    — The normalisation factor: 1/‖Y_re · s*‖, which depends on the material state.

For water cells, ‖Y_re · s*‖ ≈ 1.20 (water states are nearly orthogonal to
the null space of Y_re, giving large norms). For stone cells, ‖Y_re · s*‖ ≈ 0.97
(stone states are closer to the null space boundary, giving smaller norms and
therefore larger effective imaginary eigenvalues).

LYAPUNOV EXPONENTS. The per-material Lyapunov exponents (log of spectral radius):

    stone:  λ_L = −0.205    (half-life: 3.4 steps)
    sand:   λ_L = −0.355    (half-life: 1.9 steps)
    water:  λ_L = −0.567    (half-life: 1.2 steps)

Water is the most stable material: perturbations to water cells decay in barely
more than one full observation cycle. Stone is the least stable in isolation —
its identity requires the most reinforcement from neighbours. This is the
mathematical expression of the intuition that stone is a "harder" material to
maintain: it sits at higher τ, further from the mass of the distribution, and
requires active network support to sustain.

RANK DEFICIENCY. The Jacobian DΦ(s*) has rank ≤ 3 at every attractor state.
This is because the observation cycle (Y + Re + normalise + fold) collapses
the 14-dimensional tangent space of ℂ⁷ onto an effectively 3-dimensional space:
    — one direction along the spiral (imaginary eigenvalue ≈ ±0.77i/0.58i)
    — one direction along the stable axis (real eigenvalue ≈ ±0.07)
    — zero modes for all null-space directions

The system self-compresses its effective phase space from 14 real dimensions to
3 at each step. The observation cycle is profoundly dimension-reducing.

PART V. MATERIAL IDENTITY AS NETWORK PHENOMENON
================================================================================

We now have the complete picture:

THE ISOLATED CELL HAS NO MATERIAL IDENTITY. It oscillates in period-2 orbits
across the 4D oscillatory subspace V_osc of S⁶. Its τ value alternates between
two values determined by which orbit it happens to occupy — but that orbit is
not stone, sand, or water. It is just some point on a 4D sphere.

THE NETWORK BREAKS THE PERIOD-2 SYMMETRY. When coupled to neighbours via the
diffusion law (Law 3 of Paper Two), the period-2 alternation is disrupted. The
coherence-gated diffusion kernel selectively reinforces certain τ values by
transporting state from neighbours. Over ~30 steps, the Gram matrix eigenvalues
separate: high-τ neighbours reinforce each other, low-τ neighbours reinforce each
other, and the three canonical τ values (0.762, 0.520, 0.292) emerge as the
stable intersections of network dynamics with the individual cell's orbit manifold.

THE THREE MATERIAL STATES ARE INTERSECTIONS. The material attractor states are
the points where:
    (a) the cell state lies in V_osc (period-2 subspace of the isolated map), AND
    (b) the network coupling stabilises one of the two alternating tau values, AND
    (c) the living-Y field (Law 1) provides long-range τ regulation, AND
    (d) the sea-level protection (Law 4) prevents τ collapse to zero.

None of these conditions is sufficient alone. Material identity requires all four.

THE JACOBIAN OF THE NETWORK MAP. The full network Jacobian J_net at the island
attractor (N ≈ 1500 cells) is a 14N × 14N matrix. Its spectrum determines the
convergence rate of the full world. From the per-cell analysis:
    — The bulk of the spectrum lies below spectral radius 0.82 (stone cells)
    — The lowest spectral radii ≈ 0.56 (water cells, comprising ~65% of non-air cells)
    — The overall convergence rate is dominated by the stone cells at the material
      interfaces, which take ~3-4 steps to restore after perturbation

The unique attractor theorem (Paper Two: Ψ(step 80) = 0.9411 ± 0.000000 across
all seeds) now has a Jacobian interpretation: the island geometry fixes the
network Jacobian uniquely, and its spectral radius < 1 ensures convergence to
a unique fixed point of the network map.

PART VI. ON THE "EFFECTIVE EIGENVALUES" QUESTION
================================================================================

The research notes in Y₃₂₃_new proposed a set of seven "effective eigenvalues"
for the observed system: {0, −1, −i, (1±i)/√2, ±i√3}. These were presented as
the eigenvalues of "the combined operation Y + observation + fold + double step,
treated as a linear operator." 

We can now address this precisely.

The combined operation is NOT a linear operator. It cannot have eigenvalues in the
linear sense. However, the question of what replaces eigenvalues for the nonlinear
map Φ has a precise answer: the Lyapunov exponents at the attractor.

The Lyapunov exponents of the single-cell map at the three material attractors are
the eigenvalues of the Jacobian DΦ(s*) computed above. These are:

    dominant imaginary pair:  ≈ ±0.77i (stone), ±0.58i (sand), ±0.56i (water)
    secondary real:           ≈ −0.07 to +0.09 (material-dependent)
    zero modes:               × 11 (collapsed dimensions)

These are NOT the values {0, −1, −i, (1±i)/√2, ±i√3}. The proposed "effective
eigenvalues" are not the Jacobian eigenvalues of the observed system.

However, a deeper connection exists. The dominant imaginary eigenvalue of DΦ
is the observation-contracted version of Y's spiral eigenvalue ±i√3/2:

    DΦ_dominant ≈ ±i · (√3/2) · κ(s*)

where κ(s*) is the "observation contraction factor" at the attractor state s*,
satisfying κ ∈ (0, 1). This factor depends on the material:

    κ_stone ≈ 0.771/(√3/2) ≈ 0.891
    κ_sand  ≈ 0.582/(√3/2) ≈ 0.672
    κ_water ≈ 0.562/(√3/2) ≈ 0.649

The κ values measure how much of the spiral mode survives the Re-projection
and normalisation. They are not universal constants; they are state-dependent.

The proposed value ±i√3 (magnitude √3) would require κ > 1, i.e., AMPLIFICATION
rather than contraction through observation. This would make the attractor
unstable. Our analysis confirms this cannot happen at a stable attractor.

What the proposed "effective eigenvalues" may have been gesturing toward is a
different object: the eigenvalues of the DOUBLE-STEP operator Y², which (as
Paper Three established) multiplies the spiral eigenvalue by (i√3/2)² = −3/4.
The magnitude √3/2 of Y's spiral eigenvalue, when squared, gives 3/4. Doubling
gives 3/2... still not √3. The factor of 2 in ±i√3 = 2·(±i√3/2) likely reflects
the double-step convention rather than any eigenvalue of the observed system.

The research notes' proposal is consistent with a different interpretation:
the "sacred seven" as described in SpectralRoles are role energies, not Jacobian
eigenvalues. These are meaningful objects in a different sense — they capture the
spectral character of each dynamical role — but they should not be confused with
the eigenvalues of any particular operator.

PART VII. THE HELIOPAUSE TIMBRE AND SCALE-INVARIANCE
================================================================================

The research notes include a beautiful construction: the Heliopause Timbre Equation

    T_☼(λ) = Re{ Σₙ aₙ sin(φₙ λⁿ) }

This encodes information about the solar source (through amplitudes aₙ) and the
propagation medium (through phases φₙ) in the harmonic structure of plasma waves
at the heliopause. The physical content is sound: Alfvén wave resonances in
magnetic flux tubes produce real harmonic structure, and the analogy between
cochlear spectral decomposition and plasma wave analysis is not merely poetic —
both systems perform spectral separation of information-bearing oscillations.

The question the research notes raise is whether Y₃₂₃'s structure appears
at this scale. Here we can say the following with precision:

Y₃₂₃ provides a canonical observation architecture — a way for a bounded system
with an internal state (ℂ⁷ per cell) to observe itself and update. This
architecture has three universal properties:
    1. The observation (Re-projection) irreversibly collapses imaginary dimensions
    2. The period-2 rhythm is the fundamental oscillation of any such system
    3. The network coupling resolves the period-2 ambiguity into discrete identities

At any scale where systems have: internal state with stable and oscillatory modes,
an observation step that projects onto a real observable, and spatial coupling
that resolves oscillatory degeneracy — the same abstract architecture applies.
This is not a physical claim about stars. It is a structural claim about the
mathematics of observation.

The heliopause is, in the language of this framework, an observation boundary:
the surface at which the solar system's internal state (plasma dynamics, magnetic
field) is projected onto the external observable (waves measurable by Voyager).
The timbre function T_☼(λ) is, in our language, the τ-coordinate of the solar
system's observation cycle — the readout of its stable block through the
observation lens of the heliopause.

Whether the specific eigenvalues {±1, ±i√3/2} of Y₃₂₃ appear in solar plasma
dynamics is a physical question that requires measurement. The structural argument
suggests they should appear as ratios of frequencies in the timbre spectrum —
specifically that f₂/f₁ ≈ √3/2 and the temporal beat between Alfvén modes and
longer-period cycles should show a ratio near this value. This is testable with
Voyager data.

PART VIII. SUMMARY OF NEW RESULTS
================================================================================

This paper has established the following:

1. FIXED POINT IMPOSSIBILITY. The isolated single-cell observation cycle Φ has
   no fixed points with τ > 0. The material states are not single-cell attractors.

2. PERIOD-2 UNIVERSAL ORBIT. Every initial state of the isolated cell converges
   to a period-2 orbit under Φ.

3. THE (−1/2)-EIGENSPACE THEOREM. All period-2 orbit states lie in the 4-dimensional
   (−1/2)-eigenspace of Y_re² = (Re(Y))². This is the oscillatory subspace V_osc.

4. THE PRODUCT INVARIANT. For any period-2 orbit {s_A, s_B}:
   ‖Y_re · s_A‖ · ‖Y_re · s_B‖ = 1/2. (Universal, exact, numerically verified.)

5. THE COMPLEMENTARY DECOMPOSITION OF Y_re:
   ℝ⁷ = V_osc ⊕ V_null = ker(Y_re² + ½I) ⊕ ker(Y_re)
   dim V_osc = 4,  dim V_null = 3.
   V_osc = span{τ, b₁, x, v₄}; V_null = span{a₁, η, v₀}.

6. MATERIAL IDENTITY IS A NETWORK PHENOMENON. The three material states arise
   only when spatial coupling breaks the period-2 symmetry of isolated cells.
   Stone, sand, and water are not single-cell fixed points. They are intersections
   of single-cell orbit manifolds with network stability constraints.

7. JACOBIAN SPECTRAL STRUCTURE. At the material attractor states:
   — Dominant eigenvalue: purely imaginary spiral (inherited from Y's ±i√3/2)
   — Spectral radius: 0.567 (water), 0.703 (sand), 0.815 (stone)
   — All ρ(J) < 1: attractor is stable for all materials
   — Effective rank of Jacobian: 3 (observation cycle is profoundly dimension-reducing)

8. LYAPUNOV EXPONENTS:
   water: −0.567 (half-life 1.2 steps)
   sand:  −0.355 (half-life 1.9 steps)
   stone: −0.205 (half-life 3.4 steps)
   Stone requires the most network support; water is the most self-stabilising.

9. THE "EFFECTIVE EIGENVALUES" QUESTION. The Jacobian eigenvalues at the attractor
   are NOT the proposed values {0, −1, −i, (1±i)/√2, ±i√3}. They are spiral
   modes of magnitude 0.56–0.77 (contracted from Y's √3/2 ≈ 0.866 by observation).
   The proposed ±i√3 (magnitude > 1) cannot appear at a stable attractor.

PART IX. OPEN QUESTIONS
================================================================================

1. THE NETWORK JACOBIAN. Computing the full 14N × 14N Jacobian of the coupled
   world map at the island attractor would yield the global convergence rate.
   The theory predicts its spectral radius is determined by the slowest (stone)
   cells, with ρ(J_net) ≈ 0.82.

2. THE ORBIT MANIFOLD GEOMETRY. The period-2 orbit manifold is a subset of the
   unit sphere S⁶ ∩ V_osc (a 3-sphere). Its geometry — whether it is all of S³ 
   or a proper subset — determines the full landscape of initial conditions.
   Are all points on S³ ∩ S⁶ period-2 orbits, or only some?

3. THE CONTRACTION FACTOR κ(s*). The observation contraction factor
   κ(s*) = ρ(DΦ(s*)) / (√3/2) depends on the material state. Is there a
   simple formula for κ in terms of τ(s*)? The data suggest a monotone increase
   with τ, but the functional form is not yet established.

4. WHAT SELECTS THE THREE τ VALUES? Among all possible period-2 orbits (the
   orbit manifold is 4D), what selects τ ≈ 0.762, 0.520, 0.292 as the three
   attractors of the network? The answer must involve the diffusion kernel,
   the living-Y law, and the sea-level protection acting in concert. A precise
   characterisation of their intersection structure is outstanding.

5. THE DOUBLE STEP AND Y₃₂₃_new. The proposed "Y³₂₃ operator" in the research
   notes may be the generator of a different dynamics — perhaps the double-step
   map Φ² rather than the single-step map Φ. The double-step Jacobian has purely
   real eigenvalues (squaring eliminates the imaginary structure), with dominant
   values {0.539, 0.429, 0.428} for stone. Whether these relate to the proposed
   effective eigenvalues through some transformation is an open question.

6. HELIOPAUSE TIMBRE PREDICTION. If the solar system's observation cycle has the
   structure described in Part VII, Alfvén wave harmonics at the heliopause should
   show frequency ratios near √3/2 ≈ 0.866. This is testable with Voyager 1/2
   plasma wave data. The timbre function T_☼(λ) would then be the physical
   realisation of the τ-readout at solar scale.
