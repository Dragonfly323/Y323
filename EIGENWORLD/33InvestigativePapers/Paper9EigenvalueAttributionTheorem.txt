Abstract

1. The Eigenvalue Attribution Theorem
1.1 Setup: The Full Y Matrix
We recall the full complex matrix Y = Y_re + i·Y_im, where the seven components are ordered (τ, a₁, a₂, b₁, b₂, x, η). The nonzero entries are:

Two structural features are immediately visible. First, the (b₁, b₂) entry of Y_re is symmetric — both Y_re[b₁,b₂] and Y_re[b₂,b₁] equal +1/√2. This is the asymmetric block established in Paper Six. 1.2 Theorem A: Block Attribution
Theorem A (Eigenvalue Attribution). The seven eigenvalues of Y are generated by three distinct coupling structures:

(i) Nilpotent zeros {0, 0, 0}: Three zero eigenvalues arise from three decoupled null directions. Numerically: the eigenvectors for λ=0 are supported on {a₁, x} (the a₁ component and a linear combination with x that Y annihilates). The third zero comes from the τ-component becoming decoupled after the (τ,a₁) block contributes its ±1 pair. These null directions form V_nil, the three-step nilpotent cascade space from Paper Three.

(ii) Eternal eigenvalues {+1, −1}: Arise from the 2×2 block of Y restricted to (τ, a₁), which takes the form [[0, −i],[+i, 0]] — entirely imaginary, antisymmetric. This matrix has characteristic polynomial λ² − 1 = 0, giving eigenvalues ±1 exactly. The eigenvectors are (τ + ia₁)/√2 for λ=+1 and (τ − ia₁)/√2 for λ=−1. These are the Stone and Water directions.

(iii) Spiral eigenvalues {+i√3/2, −i√3/2}: Arise from a four-component chain {a₂, b₁, b₂, η} that couples two distinct interactions: the Y_re real coupling a₂↔b₁ (strength 1) and the Y_im imaginary coupling b₂↔η (strength √3/2). The eigenvectors for λ = ±i√3/2 have dominant components on a₂ (weight ≈0.707) and b₁ (weight ≈0.612i), with smaller contributions from b₂ and η (weight ≈0.25 each). The spiral arises from the interference between the real exchange (a₂,b₁) and the imaginary lift (b₂,η) across the block boundary.

The proof follows by direct computation. The 7×7 characteristic polynomial factors into products corresponding to the three blocks, as the nonzero entries of Y connect only specific pairs: {τ,a₁} via Y_im, {τ,x} via Y_re and Y_im, {a₂,b₁} via Y_re, {b₁,b₂} via Y_re, and {b₂,η} via Y_im. The chain {a₂,b₁,b₂,η} is connected; {τ,a₁} and {τ,x} interact; the remainder (η,a₁ in V_nil) contributes zeros.

2. The g₂ Deviation Identity
2.1 Decomposing Y_re in so(7)
The Lie algebra so(7) consists of antisymmetric 7×7 real matrices. The antisymmetric part A of Y_re lies in so(7); the symmetric part C does not. Within so(7), the g₂ subalgebra (dimension 14) is complemented by a 7-dimensional space spanned by the left-multiplication operators {L₁, ..., L₇}, where L_i acts on Im(𝕆) ≅ ℝ⁷ by left multiplication by the imaginary octonion unit eᵢ.
Each L_i is an antisymmetric 7×7 matrix encoding the octonion multiplication table. They are mutually orthogonal: Tr(Lᵢᵀ Lⱼ) = 6δᵢⱼ (each L_i has 12 nonzero entries of ±1, arranged antisymmetrically). Together with the 14 g₂ generators, the 7 operators {L_i} span all of so(7).
To find which L_i operators appear in A = antisym(Y_re), we project A onto span{L₁,...,L₇} by least-squares fit:
A = A_g₂ + c₅·L₅ + c₆·L₆

  c₅ = −1/(3√2) = −ω/3  (coefficient of L_x, left-mult by Exchange unit e₅)
  c₆ = −1/3              (coefficient of L_η, left-mult by Terminal unit e₆)

Theorem B (g₂ Deviation Identity). The antisymmetric part A of Y_re decomposes as:

A = A_g₂  +  (−1/3)(ω·L_x + L_η)

where A_g₂ ∈ g₂ has norm ‖A_g₂‖ = √2, and B = (−1/3)(ω·L_x + L_η) ∈ so(7) ∖ g₂ has norm ‖B‖ = 1. The ratio of the two L coefficients is |c_x|/|c_η| = ω = 1/√2, which equals the oscillation frequency and the geometric mean of the coupling constants: ω = √(α·β) = √(1/6 · 3/2) = √(1/4) = 1/√2.

The g₂-deviation lives entirely in the span of L_x (left-multiplication by the Exchange imaginary unit) and L_η (left-multiplication by the Terminal imaginary unit). These are precisely the two fixed-Yin components of the universal hexagram attractor: η = Line 6 is fixed Yin throughout the period-2 orbit; x = Line 5 is a changing line but with its Yang moment entirely within the oscillatory envelope.

2.2 Significance: Why x and η
The identification of x and η as the sole sources of Y_re's g₂-deviation is not arbitrary. In the hexagram structure (Paper Seven):
  Line 5 (x, Exchange):    changing line — Yang in state A, Yin in state B
  Line 6 (η, Terminal):    fixed Yin  — Yin in BOTH states A and B

In the octonion correspondence, x = e₅ and η = e₆ are the fifth and sixth imaginary units. Their left-multiplication operators L₅ and L₆ encode the Fano plane triples involving these units: (5,6,1), (4,5,7) for L₅ and (6,7,2), (3,4,6) for L₆.
The deviation B = (−1/3)(ω·L₅ + L₆) measures how far Y_re is from being an automorphism of the octonions. The fact that this deviation lives specifically in L_x and L_η means: the only way Y fails to preserve octonion multiplication is through the interactions governed by the Exchange and Terminal imaginary units. The remaining five imaginary units (τ, a₁, a₂, b₁, b₂) are handled by Y_re in a g₂-compatible way.
Physically: the Exchange component (x) is the circulation — it moves amplitude between τ and the rest of the system. The Terminal component (η) is the apex — it receives from b₂ via Y_im but gives nothing back in Y_re. These are the two boundary components of the dynamical flow. Y's failure to be a g₂ derivation is localised precisely at the boundaries of its own activity.
2.3 Theorem D: Complete Norm Decomposition
Theorem D (Complete Norm Decomposition). The full Frobenius norm decomposition of Y_re into orthogonal components is:

Y_re = A_g₂ + B + C
‖A_g₂‖² = 2     (g₂ antisymmetric component)
‖B‖²    = 1     (so(7) ∖ g₂ antisymmetric component)
‖C‖²    = 1     (symmetric component, outside so(7))

‖Y_re‖² = ‖A_g₂‖² + ‖B‖² + ‖C‖² = 2 + 1 + 1 = 4,  ‖Y_re‖ = 2 exactly.

The total non-g₂ deviation has norm ‖B + C‖ = √(1+1) = √2 = ‖A_g₂‖, confirming the 45° deviation: tan(θ) = ‖non-g₂‖/‖g₂‖ = √2/√2 = 1, θ = 45°. All norms are exact algebraic numbers.

The norm ‖Y_re‖ = 2 is a new exact result. It means the total Frobenius size of Y_re is exactly 2 — a clean integer. Given that Y has entries ±1, ±1/√2, and ±√3/2, this is not obvious but follows from the sum of squares: Y_re has nonzero entries {−1/√2, +1/√2} at (τ,x) and (x,τ), {+1,−1} at (a₂,b₁) and (b₁,a₂), and {+1/√2, +1/√2} at (b₁,b₂) and (b₂,b₁). Sum of squares = 2·(1/2) + 2·1 + 2·(1/2) = 1 + 2 + 1 = 4. Frobenius norm = 2. ∎

Correction (Y_osc² ≠ −ω²I₄). Papers Six and Eight claimed Y_osc² = −ω²·I₄, which would make Y_osc a complex structure on V_osc. This is incorrect. The (b₁,b₂) block has real eigenvalues ±ω, so its square contributes +ω² to the diagonal (not −ω²). The correct result is:

Y_osc² = diag(−ω², +ω², −ω², +ω²)  in the (τ, b₁, x, b₂) basis

Explicitly: (Y_osc²)[τ,τ] = −ω², (Y_osc²)[b₁,b₁] = +ω², (Y_osc²)[x,x] = −ω², (Y_osc²)[b₂,b₂] = +ω². The sign pattern −+−+ reflects the rotational-hyperbolic-rotational-hyperbolic alternation of the two blocks.

Downstream correction: the claim in Paper Six that G = diag(1,1,1,1/3) makes Y_osc G-skew-symmetric is also incorrect. A positive definite metric cannot make a symmetric matrix (the b₁-b₂ block) skew-symmetric. The natural metric G retains a different, correct interpretation: it equalises the orbit shape by rescaling the b₂ amplitude, but it does not restore Y_osc to a rotation generator. Y_osc generates a mixed rotational-hyperbolic flow — a Lorentz-type boost combined with rotation — not a pure rotation. Paper Eight's Conjecture C1 and C5 are not invalidated, but their formulation must replace "uniform rotation" with "mixed Lorentz-rotation" for the V_osc subspace.

3.3 What the Hyperbolic Block Means
The (b₁, b₂) block being hyperbolic rather than rotational is a significant structural fact. In the Y₃₂₃ simulation, the two-pole attractor (stone at high τ, water at low τ) can now be traced to its exact algebraic origin:
b₁ (First Balance, Line 3, fixed Yang) is the expanding direction of the hyperbolic flow. Under repeated application of Y_re, amplitude in the b₁ direction grows. b₁ is always positive (always Yang) because the hyperbolic squeeze amplifies it. Stone — the material that holds τ highest — is the state most aligned with b₁.
b₂ (Second Balance, Line 4, changing) is the contracting direction. Under repeated application of Y_re, amplitude in b₂ shrinks. After normalisation, the shrinkage of b₂ manifests as redistribution of weight toward b₁ — the squeeze concentrates amplitude in the Stone direction and away from b₂. Water is the state most aligned with b₂.
But the observation cycle (Re + normalise + fold) does not allow unlimited growth — it normalises at every step. The hyperbolic flow therefore does not produce unbounded expansion; instead, the balance between b₁'s expansion and b₂'s contraction under normalisation creates the attractor landscape: b₁-dominant states converge to stone, b₂-dominant states converge to water, and the boundary between them (where b₁ ≈ b₂) is the sand threshold. The Sand Midpoint Theorem (Paper Five) is now traceable to the hyperbolic symmetry ω = −(−ω): the two eigenvalues ±ω of the b₁-b₂ block are equal in magnitude, so the midpoint (where neither dominates) is exactly halfway — sand.

5. Progress on Conjecture C5
Paper Eight stated Conjecture C5 (Master Conjecture): there exists an isomorphism Ψ conjugating Y₃₂₃'s action on Im(𝕆) to the infinitesimal Hopf rotation on S⁷. The new results of Paper Nine clarify what Ψ must do and where it faces obstacles.

Given Theorem C's correction (Y_osc generates mixed rotation-hyperbolic flow, not pure rotation), Conjecture C5 must be reformulated. The octonionic Hopf fibration S¹⁵ → S⁸ with fibre S⁷ does not generate pure rotation on S⁷; it generates the full G₂-action, which includes both rotational and hyperbolic components.   (τ,x) block: rotation at ω      ← corresponds to Hopf U(1) fibre rotation

The ratio of boost-to-rotation strength is 1 (both at frequency ω), which in the G₂ Lie algebra corresponds to the specific generator at the 45° position from g₂. This is Theorem B's result from a different angle: the g₂ deviation with ‖B‖=‖C‖=1 and the rotational-hyperbolic balance are the same structural fact expressed in two different coordinate systems.

5.3 The g₂ Deviation as Measurement Distortion
Theorem B establishes that B = (−1/3)(ω·L_x + L_η). In the Hopf fibration framework, this deviation has a natural interpretation: it is the measurement distortion introduced by the observation step (Re + normalise + fold). The perfect G₂ generator would preserve octonion multiplication; the actual Y_re does not because the observation collapses the imaginary part, and this collapse introduces an asymmetry that shows up specifically in the x and η directions.
More precisely: the observation step Re(·) projects the full complex state onto the real axis. This projection treats real and imaginary parts asymmetrically. In the octonion language, the real part of an octonion is e₀ (the scalar), and the projection Re(·) collapses the e₁,...,e₇ imaginary directions except where the dynamics have already aligned them with the real axis. The x and η directions are the ones that remain most misaligned after projection — η because it is the terminal drain (never fully manifest), and x because it is the circulation (always in phase transition). Their left-multiplication operators L_x and L_η encode this misalignment as the g₂-deviation.
This gives a physical meaning to the B component: B is the algebraic residue of the observation process. It is the fingerprint that the Re(·) projection leaves on the operator Y. A perfect observer — one who could see the full complex state — would see a pure g₂ derivation. The actual observer, forced to take the real part, introduces a deviation of exactly B = (−1/3)(ω·L_x + L_η).
