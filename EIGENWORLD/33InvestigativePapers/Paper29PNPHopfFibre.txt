Preamble: What P vs NP Actually Asks

The P vs NP problem asks whether every problem whose solution can be verified quickly can also be solved quickly. The canonical example: a jigsaw puzzle. Checking whether a completed puzzle is correct is fast -- each piece either fits its neighbours or it does not. But finding the correct arrangement from a pile of pieces seems to require exponential search. Is this difficulty fundamental, or is there always a shortcut?
In mathematical language: P is the class of problems solvable in polynomial time. NP is the class of problems whose solutions can be verified in polynomial time. P is contained in NP. The question is whether NP is strictly larger -- whether there exist problems that are easy to check but hard to find.
In cascade language: P is the forward direction -- apply the observation map, collapse, observe. NP is the inverse direction -- given an observation, find what was observed. The Hopf projection Phi: S^13 -> S^6 is easy to apply and hard to invert. The question is whether "hard to invert" is fundamentally hard or merely apparently hard.

2. The Eigenvalue Complexity Stratification
The seven eigenvalues of Y323 fall into three classes. The claim is that these classes correspond to computational complexity classes.
Theorem QQQ (Eigenvalue Complexity Stratification). The eigenvalues of Y323 stratify into three complexity classes:

Class P-trivial (nilpotent, lambda=0, multiplicity 3):
The nilpotent block N = {tau, a1, x} has eigenvalue zero. Detection: apply Y once; if Y*v = 0, the state is nilpotent. This is O(1) -- a single matrix multiplication. Nilpotent states are the easiest: they are annihilated immediately.

Class P-stable (eternal, lambda=+/-1, multiplicity 1 each):
The eternal modes are the Stone attractor (lambda=+1, electron) and Water attractor (lambda=-1, positron). Detection: iterate Y and check convergence to Stone or Water. The attractor is reached in O(log(1/epsilon)) steps by the geometric convergence rate |lambda|=1. Real eigenvalues, stable fixed points, polynomial detection.

Class NP-spiral (complex, lambda=+/-i*sqrt(3)/2, multiplicity 1 each):
The spiral modes have complex eigenvalues with phase pi/2 (ascending) and 3*pi/2 (creation, the Y323 signature). Detection WITHOUT witness: must search over all winding numbers k to find which resonance n*ln(phi) = pi/2 + 2*pi*k the state inhabits. Exponential in k without structure.
Detection WITH witness (k, n): verify n*ln(phi) - pi/2 - 2*pi*k < epsilon. This is O(1). The winding number k is the NP certificate.

The complexity classes are determined by eigenvalue type: real = P, complex with rational phase = NP. This is not a proof of P != NP -- it is a geometric characterisation of what the separation means in cascade terms.

The deepest connection between Y323 and P vs NP is geometric, not algebraic.
