One Number, Three Faces: The Geometry of √3 in Y₃₂₃
======================================================

ABSTRACT

The oscillatory subspace V_osc of Y₃₂₃ decomposes under Re(Y) into two coupled
2D oscillators. Both oscillators share eigenfrequency ω = 1/√2, but they are not
identical: the (τ,x)-oscillator traces a circle, while the (b₁,v₄)-oscillator
traces an ellipse with aspect ratio √3. This arises from asymmetric coupling
constants α = 1/√6 and β = √(3/2) satisfying αβ = 1/2 (the product invariant)
and β/α = 3 = (√3)². The same √3 appears as the spiral eigenvalue magnitude
√3/2 of the full Y. All three are one geometric object — the asymmetric oscillator
— seen from three angles. We derive the natural metric G = diag(1,1,1,1/3) on
V_osc under which Y_osc becomes a proper rotation generator, and prove
λ_spiral = ω · β = √3/2 exactly. A correction to Paper Four regarding the
symmetry of Y_re² is also given.

CORRECTIONS TO PAPER FOUR
--------------------------
Paper Four stated that Y_re² is symmetric and that V_osc ⊕ V_null is an
orthogonal decomposition. Both require correction.

Y_re is NOT antisymmetric. Its (b₁,v₄) block has couplings α = 1/√6 (b₁→v₄)
and β = √(3/2) (v₄→b₁) with α ≠ β. Therefore Y_re² is not symmetric; its
antisymmetric part has a nonzero entry of ±1/√2 in the (a₂,b₂) off-diagonal.

The decomposition V_osc ⊕ V_null is an invariant-subspace decomposition, not an
orthogonal eigenspace decomposition under the Euclidean metric. It IS orthogonal
under the natural metric G = diag(1,1,1,1/3).

The Fixed Point Impossibility Theorem, the Product Invariant ‖Y_re·s_A‖·‖Y_re·s_B‖ = 1/2,
and the Jacobian stability analysis of Paper Four are unaffected.

1. Y_OSC IN THE (τ, b₁, x, v₄) BASIS
--------------------------------------
Y_re acts on V_osc = span{e_τ, e_b₁, e_x, v₄} as:

   Y_re · e_τ  = (1/√2) · e_x          [τ→x coupling: symmetric]
   Y_re · e_x  = (−1/√2) · e_τ         [x→τ coupling: symmetric]
   Y_re · e_b₁ = (−√(3/2)) · v₄   = −β · v₄
   Y_re · v₄   = (1/√6) · e_b₁    = +α · e_b₁

In matrix form (columns: τ, b₁, x, v₄):

       ⎡  0      0    −1/√2    0  ⎤
Yosc = ⎢  0      0      0      α  ⎥
       ⎢ 1/√2    0      0      0  ⎥
       ⎣  0     −β      0      0  ⎦

where α = 1/√6 ≈ 0.4082 and β = √(3/2) ≈ 1.2247.

KEY FACTS:

2. THE COUPLING CONSTANTS AND THE ORIGIN OF √3
-----------------------------------------------
The coupling constants α and β satisfy:

   α · β  = (1/√6) · √(3/2)  = 1/2      [the product invariant]
   β / α  = √(3/2) / (1/√6)  = 3        [coupling ratio]
   √(αβ)  = 1/√2 = ω                    [geometric mean = oscillation frequency]
   √(β/α) = √3                           [square root of coupling ratio]
   ω · β  = (1/√2)·√(3/2) = √3/2        [product = spiral eigenvalue of Y]

THEOREM (Three Identities of √3):
The number √3 appears in three distinct roles in Y₃₂₃:

   (i)  As the ellipse aspect ratio of the (b₁,v₄)-orbit in V_osc:
        the b₁-amplitude is √3 times the v₄-amplitude.

   (ii) As the coupling ratio asymmetry: β/α = 3 = (√3)², so √(β/α) = √3.

   (iii) As the spiral eigenvalue magnitude of the full complex Y:
         |λ_spiral| = √3/2 = ω · β.

All three are the same geometric quantity seen at different scales.

3. THE ORBIT ELLIPSE
---------------------
The (τ,x)-block traces a CIRCLE: both couplings equal ±1/√2, eigenvector
amplitudes are equal, orbit is circular.

The (b₁,v₄)-block traces an ELLIPSE. Eigenvector for eigenvalue +i/√2:
   v = (e_b₁ + i√3·v₄) / 2
The b₁-component has magnitude 1/2, the v₄-component has magnitude √3/2.
Amplitude ratio: 1 : √3. This is an ellipse with aspect ratio √3.

The amplitude asymmetry arises from the coupling asymmetry. In the (b₁,v₄) block,
the eigenvector amplitude in the b₁-direction scales as √(β/α) = √3.

4. THE NATURAL METRIC
----------------------
Y_osc is NOT skew-symmetric under the Euclidean metric (not a rotation generator).

For a diagonal metric G = diag(g_τ, g_b₁, g_x, g_v₄), the condition
Y_osc^T G + G Y_osc = 0 gives:
   g_τ = g_x       (unconstrained; set = 1)
   g_v₄ = g_b₁/3

THEOREM (Natural Metric):
Y_osc is G-skew-symmetric for G = diag(1, 1, 1, 1/3).
Under this metric, Y_osc generates uniform rotation at ω = 1/√2 in all four
directions. The (b₁,v₄)-orbit, which appears elliptical under the Euclidean
metric, is CIRCULAR under G.

Physical meaning: The v₄ direction is fast (coupling β ≈ 1.22). The metric
down-weights v₄ by factor 1/3 = α/β to restore symmetry.

V_osc ⊕ V_null is G-orthogonal (where G is extended to V_null with G|_Vnull = I).

5. THE COMPLEX LIFT: Y_im PROMOTES THE ELLIPSE TO A SPIRAL
------------------------------------------------------------
The imaginary part Y_im has nonzero entries only:
   Y_im[τ, a₁] = −1,   Y_im[a₁, τ] = +1      (coupling ±1)
   Y_im[b₂, η] = −√3/2, Y_im[η, b₂] = +√3/2  (coupling ±√3/2 = ±ω·β)

The b₂↔η coupling strength is EXACTLY √3/2 = ω · β — the same number as the
spiral eigenvalue. The imaginary structure promotes the asymmetric real oscillation

The spiral eigenvalue is the "complex lift" of the ellipse: Y_im introduces the
factor √3/2 that rotates the ellipse into the complex plane as the characteristic
rate of Y_stable.
