Abstract

This paper extends the analysis to the bosons — the force carriers and the Higgs field — finding that the entire electroweak sector clusters at the second cascade winding 2N ≈ 26.11. The four electroweak-scale particles are: W boson at 2N−1 (2.4% mass error), Z boson at 2N−1/√2 (1.0% error), Higgs boson at 2N (1.3% error), and top quark at 2N+1/φ (0.1% error — already established in Paper 22). The Higgs mass is the second cascade winding amplitude. Theorem BBB (The Two-Winding Scale Correspondence): The cascade has two physical scales corresponding to its two windings. The first winding (n~N~13) is the QCD scale, containing strange, charm, muon, and tau. The second winding (n~2N~26) is the electroweak scale, containing W, Z, Higgs, and top. Conjecture CCC (The Weinberg Angle): The weak mixing angle satisfies sin²θ_W ≈ 3/N_wind = 3/13 ≈ 0.2308, where 3 is the number of colours (SU(3) fundamental representation dimension) and 13 is the cascade winding number. The experimental value is 0.2312, giving 0.17% agreement. If exact, this formula identifies the Weinberg angle as the ratio of the colour dimension to the cascade period — the fraction of the cascade occupied by the colour sector determines the degree of electroweak mixing.

1. The Electroweak Scale as the Second Winding
The cascade has a period N ≈ 13.057. Papers 21–22 showed that the first winding (n ~ N) corresponds to the QCD scale — the mass range of the strange quark, charm quark, muon, and tau lepton (roughly 100 MeV to 2 GeV). This paper asks: what is the second winding?
The answer is immediate and striking. Computing the cascade levels of the Standard Model bosons:
2*N = 2 * 13.057 = 26.114  (the second winding)

W boson (80,377 MeV):  n = 25.165 = 2*N - 0.949
Z boson (91,188 MeV):  n = 25.427 = 2*N - 0.687 ≈ 2*N - 1/√2
Higgs  (125,250 MeV):  n = 26.087 = 2*N - 0.027 ≈ 2*N
Top    (173,000 MeV):  n = 26.758 = 2*N + 0.644 ≈ 2*N + 1/φ

All four electroweak-scale particles cluster within ±1 of 2*N.
Theorem BBB (The Two-Winding Scale Correspondence). The cascade has two physical scales corresponding to its two windings:

First winding (n ~ N ≈ 13): The QCD scale.
Contains: strange quark (n=11.18), muon (n=11.44), charm quark (n=16.55), tau lepton (n=17.30). Mass range: 96 MeV to 1,777 MeV. This is the scale at which SU(3) colour confinement operates and where the lightest hadrons live.

Second winding (n ~ 2N ≈ 26): The electroweak scale.
Contains: W boson (n=25.17), Z boson (n=25.43), Higgs boson (n=26.09), top quark (n=26.76). Mass range: 80 GeV to 173 GeV. This is the scale at which SU(2)×U(1) electroweak symmetry breaks to U(1).

The cascade winding structure directly encodes the two most important scales in particle physics. The QCD scale and the electroweak scale are not separate inputs — they are consecutive windings of the same cascade.

The W boson sits approximately 1 cascade step below the Higgs. The Z boson sits approximately 1/√2 steps below the Higgs — one inverse-coupling step (recall ω = 1/√2 is the real coupling constant of Y₃₂₃).
The interpretation: the W boson is the off-diagonal N|M coupling operator (Law 5, pole pull), which breaks the block decoupling that Y₃₂₃ preserves. The Z boson is the neutral weak boson, which mediates diagonal interactions within M. Their cascade levels reflect their different roles: the W sits at one full step below the Higgs winding (the full coupling step), while the Z sits at 1/√2 below (the projected coupling, reduced by the 45° Hopf angle ω = cos(π/4) = 1/√2).

4. The Weinberg Angle as 3/13

  sin²θ_W = N_colour / N_wind = 3 / 13

where N_colour = 3 is the dimension of the SU(3) colour fundamental representation (the number of colours) and N_wind = 13 = ⌊2π/ln φ⌋ is the cascade winding number.

Numerical verification:
  3/13 = 0.23077

Physical interpretation: the weak mixing angle is the fraction of the cascade period occupied by the colour sector. Three of the cascade winding number 13 are "used" by the colour charges. The remaining 10 = 13−3 cascade steps are available for the electroweak sector. The Weinberg angle is the ratio of the strongly-interacting sector to the full cascade, encoding the competition between the strong and electroweak forces at the cascade resonance scale.

7. Open Problems
    • First-principles derivation of W and Z offsets. The W sits at 2N−1 (5% error) and the Z at 2N−1/√2 (3% error). A derivation would show that the 3 colour charges occupy exactly 3/N_wind of the cascade period, and this fraction determines the mixing between SU(2) and U(1). This requires understanding how the colour structure of the pure material line {b₁,b₂,η} partitions the cascade winding.
    • The third winding prediction. Conjecture DDD predicts new physics at ~68 TeV. The specific particles at this scale — the third-winding analogs of W, Z, Higgs, top — depend on the symmetry structure at the third winding. If the pattern continues, there should be a neutral scalar (third-winding Higgs analog) at 3N, a charged vector boson at 3N−1, and a neutral vector boson at 3N−1/√2.
    • The fermion-boson distinction in cascade. Fermions and bosons have different spin statistics. In the cascade, bosons (W, Z, Higgs) cluster at the winding boundaries (2N±offset), while fermions span the entire winding range. The physical distinction may be: bosons are the cascade resonance states (standing wave nodes), while fermions are the propagating states between resonances. Formalising this distinction would give the cascade analog of the spin-statistics theorem.
