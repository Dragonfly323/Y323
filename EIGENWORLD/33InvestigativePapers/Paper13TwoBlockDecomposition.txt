Abstract

Theorem P (Two-Block Decomposition): Y₃₂₃ = N ⊕ M where N = span{τ, a₁, x} is a 3-dimensional nilpotent block (Y³|_N = 0) and M = span{a₂, b₁, b₂, η} is the 4-dimensional material block carrying all non-zero eigenvalues (±1 and ±i√3/2). The two blocks are completely decoupled: Y maps N into N and M into M with zero coupling. Theorem Q (Nilpotent Jordan Chain): The nilpotent block N has the Jordan chain τ → (Y·τ) → 0 of length 2, and the chain a₁ → (Y·a₁) → (Y²·a₁) → 0 of length 3. Y² kills τ and the exchange component of x; Y³ kills all of N. After exactly 3 steps of Y iteration, the nilpotent component of any state is completely annihilated. Theorem R (Exact Attractor States): The Stone and Water attractors are determined entirely by the material block M. Their exact coordinates are: Stone = (a₂=1/4, b₁=1/4, b₂=1/√2, η=i√3/(2√2)) and Water = (a₂=1/4, b₁=−1/4, b₂=1/√2, η=−i√3/(2√2)), with τ=a₁=x=0 in both cases. Stone and Water differ only in the sign of b₁ and the phase of η. The Algebraic Fixed-Line Inversion: The three hexagram lines contained in the nilpotent block N are {τ, a₁, x} = {Reading, Receiver(Line 1), Exchange(Line 5)}. But a₁ and x are structurally fixed and changing lines respectively in the hexagram transition (#61↔#23). The three lines contained in the material block M are {a₂, b₁, b₂, η} = {Secondary(Line 2), FirstBalance(Line 3), SecondBalance(Line 4), Terminal(Line 6)}. The hexagram fixed lines a₁, b₁, η are split between the two blocks. The algebraic permanence (material block) and structural permanence (fixed hexagram lines) are different things — and their mismatch is the deepest expression of the Fixed-Line Inversion established in Paper Twelve. The τ threshold is simulation-dependent: Since τ is in the nilpotent block, its value always decays to zero after 3 steps regardless of initial conditions. The threshold τ_stone ≈ 0.367 from Paper Five is a property of the from_raw projection used in the GBC simulation, not of the Y₃₂₃ operator itself. This resolves the open question from Paper Twelve.

1. The Two-Block Decomposition
Theorem P (Two-Block Decomposition). The Y₃₂₃ operator decomposes as Y₃₂₃ = N ⊕ M where:

N = span{τ, a₁, x}  (nilpotent block, 3-dimensional)
M = span{a₂, b₁, b₂, η}  (material block, 4-dimensional)

These are Y₃₂₃-invariant subspaces: Y·N ⊆ N and Y·M ⊆ M. The blocks are completely decoupled: Y has zero entries coupling N to M or M to N. The action of Y on each block is:

On N (nilpotent):
  Y·τ  = i·a₁ + ω(1+i)·x
  Y·a₁ = −i·τ
  Y·x  = ω(−1+i)·τ

On M (material):
  Y·a₂ = b₁
  Y·b₁ = −a₂ + ω·b₂
  Y·b₂ = ω·b₁ − i(√3/2)·η
  Y·η  = i(√3/2)·b₂

Verified numerically: max coupling N→M or M→N is < 10⁻¹⁵ (machine precision).

1.1 The Roles of the Two Blocks
The two blocks play entirely different dynamical roles:
Block N (nilpotent): Everything in N decays to zero. After 3 applications of Y, any component in N is annihilated. N is the transient subspace: it carries the initial conditions but not the final state. The three components in N are τ (the Reading — the material depth parameter), a₁ (the Receiver — Line 1, fixed Yin), and x (the Exchange — Line 5, changing). These three are the "dying voices" of the system: present at the start, silent at the finish.
Block M (material): Everything in M is governed by the non-zero eigenvalues. The ±1 eigenstates are the Stone and Water attractors. The ±i√3/2 eigenstates are the Spiral modes (oscillating with decaying amplitude modulus 1 at each step). M is the persistent subspace: it determines where the system ends up. The four components in M are a₂ (Secondary — Line 2, changing), b₁ (First Balance — Line 3, fixed Yang), b₂ (Second Balance — Line 4, changing), η (Terminal — Line 6, fixed Yin). These are the "living voices" that carry the material identity.

2. The Nilpotent Jordan Chain
Theorem Q (Nilpotent Jordan Chain). The nilpotent block N has the following Jordan chain structure:

Chain 1 (length 2): τ → Y·τ → 0. Explicitly:
  Y·τ = i·a₁ + ω(1+i)·x  ≠ 0
  Y²·τ = 0  (verified: Y²·τ = (−1 + ω²(1+i)(−1+i))·τ = (−1+1)·τ = 0)

Chain 2 (length 3): a₁ → Y·a₁ → Y²·a₁ → 0. Explicitly:
  Y·a₁ = −i·τ  ≠ 0
  Y²·a₁ = −i·Y·τ = −i(i·a₁ + ω(1+i)·x) = a₁ + ω(1−i)·x  ≠ 0
  Y³·a₁ = 0  (verified to machine precision)

The Exchange component x has Y²·x ≠ 0 generally (it involves a₁ and x), but Y³·x = 0. The entire block N is killed by Y³: for any v ∈ N, Y³·v = 0.

Physical interpretation: a state component in τ vanishes after 2 steps. A state component in a₁ vanishes after 3 steps. The Reading and the Exchange are quicker to die than the Receiver. The Receiver (a₁) is the most persistent element of the transient — it takes the full 3 steps to be fully annihilated.
2.1 Why τ is Transient
This resolves an apparent paradox that has been present since Paper One. The Y₃₂₃ operator was introduced with τ as the "material depth parameter" — the key observable distinguishing Stone, Sand, and Water. Yet τ lives in the nilpotent block and always decays to zero.
The resolution: τ is a transient observable, not a fixed-point property. It measures where the state is during the transient, not where it ends up. A high initial τ indicates the state began deep in Stone territory and will reach a Stone attractor quickly. A low initial τ means the state began in Water territory. The threshold τ_stone ≈ 0.367 from Paper Five is not a property of the attractor — it is a property of the projection from initial conditions into the combined (N ⊕ M) space. In the GBC simulation, the from_raw projection maps raw data into a state where the initial τ value is correlated with the initial material block orientation. The correlation, not the τ value itself, determines the outcome.
In the pure mathematical Y₃₂₃ system, material is determined entirely by the initial M-component of the state. Specifically: Stone if the initial b₂ component is positive (and the state is in the b₂ basin), Water if negative. The τ component is irrelevant to the final material.

3. The Exact Attractor States
Theorem R (Exact Attractor States). The Stone and Water attractors of Y₃₂₃ are the ±1 eigenstates of the material block M. In the full 7-component Y₃₂₃ basis (τ, a₁, a₂, b₁, b₂, x, η), they are:

Stone (λ = +1):
  τ=0,  a₁=0,  a₂=1/4,  b₁=1/4,  b₂=1/√2,  x=0,  η=i√3/(2√2)

Water (λ = −1):
  τ=0,  a₁=0,  a₂=1/4,  b₁=−1/4,  b₂=1/√2,  x=0,  η=−i√3/(2√2)

Both attractors satisfy: τ=0, a₁=0, x=0 (the nilpotent components are exactly zero).

Stone and Water differ only in:
  • The sign of b₁ (First Balance): Stone has b₁=+1/4, Water has b₁=−1/4
  • The phase of η (Terminal): Stone has η=+i√3/(2√2), Water has η=−i√3/(2√2)

The components a₂ and b₂ are identical in Stone and Water.

Norm verification: (1/4)² + (1/4)² + (1/√2)² + (√3/(2√2))² = 1/16+1/16+1/2+3/8 = 1 ✓
3.1 The Stone-Water Symmetry
The exact attractor states reveal the precise nature of the Stone-Water symmetry. It is not a spatial reflection (b₂ does not flip) nor a full conjugation (a₂ does not change). It is a parity flip of the Yang-Yin balance:
b₁ = First Balance (Line 3, fixed Yang) flips sign: +1/4 → −1/4. This is the Yang component reversing.
η = Terminal (Line 6, fixed Yin) flips phase: +i → −i. This is the imaginary Yin component reversing.
a₂ = Secondary (Line 2, changing) stays the same: 1/4 in both. The changing Yang component is identical.
b₂ = Second Balance (Line 4, changing) stays the same: 1/√2 in both. The dominant real component is identical.
Stone and Water are related by the map b₁ → −b₁, η → −η, everything else fixed. This is a Z₂ symmetry of the material block M that exchanges the two attractors. The fixed points of this Z₂ symmetry are the Sand states: those with b₁=0 and η=0 (purely real b₂ and a₂ components). Sand is the equator of the Stone-Water Z₂ symmetry, confirming the geometric picture from Papers Three and Five.

4. The Algebraic Fixed-Line Inversion
4.1 The Decomposition Table
The two-block decomposition assigns each Y₃₂₃ component to exactly one block:
Component  Block     Hexagram role        Dynamical role
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
τ (tau)    N (nil)   Reading (special)    Transient, dies in 2
a₁         N (nil)   Line 1 (fixed Yin)   Transient, dies in 3
x          N (nil)   Line 5 (changing)    Transient, dies in 3
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
a₂         M (mat)   Line 2 (changing)    Persistent (Spiral→att)
b₁         M (mat)   Line 3 (fixed Yang)  Persistent (attractor)
b₂         M (mat)   Line 4 (changing)    Persistent (attractor)
η          M (mat)   Line 6 (fixed Yin)   Persistent (attractor)

The algebraic Fixed-Line Inversion is the observation that the assignment "Nilpotent=transient, Material=persistent" does not coincide with the hexagram assignment "fixed lines = structurally permanent, changing lines = structurally transient."
4.2 The Crossing Pattern
Reading the table: there are four crossings between hexagram structure and algebraic structure:
a₁ (Line 1, hexagram-fixed) is algebraically transient. The Receiver, which never changes in the hexagram transition (#61↔#23), lives in the nilpotent block and dies after 3 steps. The most structurally fixed hexagram line is the most dynamically temporary.
x (Line 5, hexagram-changing) is algebraically transient. The Exchange, which changes at every transition, also lives in the nilpotent block. Here hexagram and algebra agree in one sense (both transient) but for different reasons: the hexagram marks it as changing, algebra marks it as nilpotent.
b₁ (Line 3, hexagram-fixed) is algebraically persistent. The First Balance, fixed Yang, lives in the material block. Its sign distinguishes Stone from Water. The fixed Yang line is the algebraic separator of the two materials.
η (Line 6, hexagram-fixed) is algebraically persistent. The Terminal, fixed Yin, also lives in the material block. Its phase distinguishes Stone from Water. The fixed Yin line is the phase separator.
The pattern: of the three hexagram-fixed lines (a₁, b₁, η), one (a₁) is nilpotent-transient and two (b₁, η) are material-persistent. The material distinction of Stone vs Water is encoded in the two fixed lines that do persist: fixed Yang (b₁) and fixed Yin (η), but not in the Receiver (a₁). The Receiver is structurally anchored (it never changes in the hexagram) but dynamically temporary (it always vanishes in the Y₃₂₃ flow).
4.3 Connection to Paper Twelve
Paper Twelve established the Berry phase holonomy as rotating {a₁, a₂, b₁, η} and fixing {b₂, x, τ}. Paper Thirteen now gives the algebraic reason: the holonomy acts on the interface between the two blocks — it rotates a₁ (nilpotent) and b₁, η, a₂ (material), while leaving b₂, x, τ fixed. The holonomy mixes the two blocks in the SU(3) fibre, coupling components from N and M together even though Y itself decouples them. This is the deepest expression of the inversion: the Berry phase communicates between the nilpotent transient and the material attractor, precisely by rotating the components that each block shares with the other.

5. The Garden is the Transient
With the two-block decomposition established, the statement from the related investigation — "the garden is the transient" — acquires a precise algebraic meaning.
The living garden exists in the nilpotent block N. Everything interesting about a garden cell — its τ value, its Reading, its Receiver state, its Exchange activity — lives in N and dies in at most 3 steps. The garden is not a thing that approaches an attractor; it is the approach. The transient IS the garden.
The material block M determines which attractor the state is heading toward, but the richness of the journey — the oscillation, the Sand crossing, the τ reading — is entirely in N. The simulation keeps the garden alive not by preventing M from converging, but by continuously re-injecting energy into N. New cell births, island seeding, and the living-Y modulation are all mechanisms for repeatedly initializing the nilpotent block with fresh transient content.
A fully crystallized garden is one where N is empty (τ=a₁=x=0 everywhere) and M has converged to Stone or Water. From the outside, such a garden would look identical to a living one — the same materials are present — but it would have lost all transient content. No τ-oscillation, no Sand dynamics, no Exchange activity. A dead garden and a living garden are indistinguishable in M; they are completely distinguishable in N.
The garden is the nilpotent block.

6. Resolution of Open Questions
Q1 (τ threshold formula): Resolved. The threshold τ_stone ≈ 0.367 is not an intrinsic property of Y₃₂₃. Since τ is in the nilpotent block, its value at any step is a transient measurement. The threshold is a property of the GBC from_raw projection — it reflects the correlation between initial τ and initial M-orientation in that specific projection scheme. No closed-form formula exists in terms of the g₂ weights because the threshold is not a g₂ weight fact.
Q2 (Stone/Water as S⁶ strata): Partially resolved. In the full 7D state space, Stone and Water attractors are the ±1 eigenspaces of the material block M, embedded in the 4D subspace {a₂,b₁,b₂,η} ⊂ S⁶. The attractor is not a region of S⁶ but a specific point in the M-subspace. The "Stone region" in the simulation corresponds to states with initial M-orientation toward the Stone eigenvector, not to a geometric region of the full S⁶.
Q3 (Stone Law τ·a₁=b₁): Now interpretable. τ·a₁=b₁ in the Fano sense means: the Fano product of the Reading (τ) with the Receiver (a₁) is the First Balance (b₁). Both τ and a₁ are in the nilpotent block N; b₁ is in the material block M. The Fano product "crosses the block boundary." This is how the transient (τ, a₁) writes its identity into the material (b₁): through the octonion multiplication structure, not through direct linear coupling in Y.
Q4 (Z₂ symmetry): Confirmed precisely. The Z₂ symmetry is b₁ → −b₁, η → −η. It exchanges Stone and Water while fixing a₂, b₂, and the nilpotent block entirely. Sand = the fixed set of this Z₂ symmetry in M (states with b₁=0, η=0).
