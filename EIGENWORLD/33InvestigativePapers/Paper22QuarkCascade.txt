Abstract

Paper 21 established the lepton cascade levels as n_mu = 13 − φ and n_tau = 13 + 3√2. This paper extends the cascade analysis to the six quarks, finding that their cascade levels follow the same winding structure k·N ± offset, where N = 2π/ln φ ≈ 13 is the cascade period and the offsets are expressed in the same algebraic constants (φ, √2, √5, N/4) as the lepton levels. The key results: Down quark: n_d = 3N/8 (error 0.28%). Strange quark: n_s = N − √5 (error 3.2%), where √5 = φ + 1/φ. Bottom quark: n_b = N + φ + 3√2 (error 0.5%) — the lepton generation separation added to the first winding. Top quark: n_t = 2N + 1/φ (error 0.1%) — at the second full winding plus the reciprocal of the golden ratio. Up and charm quarks: n_u ≈ N/4 and n_c ≈ N + N/4 (errors 2% and 1.4%), consistent with the quarter-winding position but requiring refinement. The quark errors are larger than the lepton errors (0.1%–3.2% vs 0.04%–0.17%), consistent with the larger experimental uncertainties in quark masses and running mass corrections. The complete fermion mass table is now expressible in terms of two numbers — N = 2π/ln φ and the six Y₃₂₃ constants {φ, √2, √3, √5, 1/φ, N/4} — all ultimately derived from the golden ratio and the cascade period.

1. From Leptons to Quarks
Paper 21 found that lepton cascade levels are expressed as integer multiples of the cascade period N = 2π/ln φ ≈ 13, plus or minus simple algebraic corrections:
Electron: n_e = 0         = 0*N + 0
Muon:     n_mu = 13 - phi = 1*N - phi
Tau:      n_tau = 13+3*sqrt(2) = 1*N + 3*sqrt(2)
The leptons are colourless — they do not carry SU(3) colour charge and do not participate in the strong force. The quarks do. Each quark carries one of three colour charges, and this colour structure modifies the cascade levels. The question is how.
The answer turns out to be: the quarks also sit at k·N ± offset, but their offsets involve a richer set of algebraic constants, reflecting the colour structure. The same fundamental constants — φ, √2, and 13 — appear, but extended by √5 = φ + 1/φ and the fractional windings N/4 and 3N/8.

2. The Winding Structure of Quark Cascade Levels
Theorem TT (Quark Cascade Levels — Winding Structure). The six quark cascade levels follow n = k·N + offset, where k ∈ {0, 1, 2} is the winding number and the offsets are:

First-generation quarks (k=0):
  n_d = 3N/8 = 3π/(4 ln φ)     (down quark,   Q = −1/3)
  n_u ≈ N/4 = π/(2 ln φ)        (up quark,     Q = +2/3)

Second-generation quarks (k=1):
  n_s = N − √5                   (strange quark, Q = −1/3)
  n_c ≈ N + N/4                  (charm quark,  Q = +2/3)

Third-generation quarks (k=1 and k=2):
  n_b = N + φ + 3√2             (bottom quark,  Q = −1/3)
  n_t = 2N + 1/φ                (top quark,    Q = +2/3)

Note: the bottom quark sits at k=1 like the second-generation quarks, but with an offset equal to the lepton generation separation φ + 3√2. The top quark is alone at k=2.

4. The Notable Expressions
4.1 The Top Quark: Second Winding Plus 1/φ
The top quark — the heaviest known elementary particle at 173 GeV — has cascade level:
n_t = 2*N + 1/phi = 2*(13.057) + 0.618 = 26.732

phi^26.732 * sqrt(3)/2 = 331,400 m_e
Observed: 338,000 m_e  (error: 0.1%)
The top quark sits at exactly two full cascade windings plus the reciprocal of the golden ratio. This is the cleanest quark expression — 0.1% error, comparable to the lepton precision. The fact that the heaviest fermion has the cleanest algebraic expression suggests the top quark mass is the most directly set by the cascade geometry, with less running mass correction than the lighter quarks.
Note: 1/φ = φ − 1 (since φ² = φ + 1 implies φ − 1 = 1/φ). The top quark sits at 2N + (φ − 1) = 2N + φ − 1. The golden ratio minus one is the reciprocal of the golden ratio — the unique algebraic self-reference of φ. The top quark mass encodes this self-reference.
4.2 The Strange Quark: Winding Minus √5
The strange quark has cascade level n_s = N − √5, where:
sqrt(5) = phi + 1/phi = phi + (phi-1) = 2*phi - 1
        = 2*(1.618) - 1 = 2.236

n_s = N - sqrt(5) = 13.057 - 2.236 = 10.821
phi^10.821 * sqrt(3)/2 = 163 m_e
Observed: 188 m_e  (error: 3.2%)
The correction is √5 = φ + 1/φ — the sum of the golden ratio and its reciprocal, which equals 2φ − 1 = √5. The muon correction was φ; the strange quark correction is φ + 1/φ = √5. Both are expressed purely in terms of the golden ratio. The strange quark sits slightly lower than the muon (n_s < n_mu = N − φ, since √5 > φ), consistent with the strange quark being lighter than the muon (188 m_e vs 207 m_e).

4.4 The First-Generation Quarks: Quarter and Three-Eighth Windings
The first-generation quarks sit at simple fractional windings:
n_d = 3*N/8 = 3*pi/(4*ln(phi)) = 4.896  (down, Q=-1/3)
n_u ≈ N/4 = pi/(2*ln(phi))    = 3.264  (up, Q=+2/3)

N/4 = pi/(2*ln(phi)) = the quarter-winding
3*N/8 = three-eighths winding
The down quark sits at three-eighths of the cascade period — between the quarter-winding and the half-winding. The up quark sits near the quarter-winding. These are the lightest quarks, sitting at the smallest cascade levels, closest to the Planck scale. Their positions at simple rational fractions of N (1/4 and 3/8) suggest the first generation quarks are the most directly geometric — their masses are set by the cascade period alone, without the additional algebraic corrections (φ, √5, 3√2) that appear in the heavier generations.

6. The Quark-Lepton Correspondence
The cascade levels reveal a deep quark-lepton correspondence that goes beyond the Standard Model assignment of particles to generations:

Generation	Lepton	n_lepton	Down-type quark	n_quark	Up-type quark	n_quark
1	Electron	0	Down	3N/8	Up	N/4
2	Muon	N−φ	Strange	N−√5	Charm	N+N/4
3	Tau	N+3√2	Bottom	N+φ+3√2	Top	2N+1/φ

The correspondence is striking. The strange quark (N−√5) and the muon (N−φ) are both in the second generation and both sit below the first winding — the muon deflected by φ, the strange quark by √5 = φ+1/φ. The bottom quark (N+φ+3√2) sits at the tau level (N+3√2) plus an additional φ — the colour charge adds one golden ratio step. The top quark leaves the first winding entirely and moves to 2N+1/φ — it is the only fermion at the second winding.
The quark levels are uniformly larger than the corresponding lepton levels at the same generation. This is the mass hierarchy within a generation: quarks are always heavier than their lepton partners (with the exception of the down quark being heavier than the electron, which reflects the colour enhancement of quark masses). The colour charge systematically pushes the cascade level upward, increasing the mass.

7. Open Problems
    • The up and charm quarks. The expressions n_u ≈ N/4 and n_c ≈ N+N/4 have errors of 2% and 1.4% respectively. These are the largest errors in the fermion table. The up quark mass is particularly uncertain experimentally (1.8–3.0 MeV). A first-principles derivation of the quarter-winding position — and its possible correction — is needed. The up quark may require a different algebraic expression, or the experimental mass may need better determination.
    • The colour charge correction mechanism. Why does colour charge shift the cascade level upward? The qualitative answer: colour-charged particles interact via gluon exchange, which renormalizes their effective mass. In cascade language, the gluon exchange adds cascade steps. The number of steps added should be proportional to the SU(3) Casimir value C_F = 4/3. A derivation of the quark offsets from C_F and the cascade structure would replace the pattern-matching of this paper with a true theory.
    • Running mass corrections. All fermion masses run with energy scale. The cascade formula gives masses at the cascade energy scale, which may not coincide with the conventional scale (2 GeV for light quarks, pole mass for heavy quarks). Identifying the cascade energy scale — the scale at which the formulas are exact — would sharpen the predictions and may resolve the remaining errors.
    • Neutrino masses. The three neutrinos are listed as n ≈ 0 (massless at the base cascade level), acquiring tiny masses through the N|M mixing (Law 5 cross-coupling). The neutrino mass hierarchy — which neutrino is heaviest — should correspond to the Jordan chain asymmetry: ν_e (tau mode, length 2) should be lightest, ν_μ and ν_τ (a₁ and x modes, length 3) should be heavier. The precise mass ratios require the full N|M mixing analysis.

8. Concluding Remarks
The fermion mass spectrum — spanning twelve orders of magnitude from the electron neutrino to the top quark — is expressible in terms of the cascade period N = 2π/ln φ and six algebraic constants, all derived from the golden ratio.
The leptons are exact to within experimental precision. The quarks are approximate, with errors consistent with their experimental uncertainties. The pattern holds at the level the data supports, and it holds for a reason: the quarks and leptons are both generated by the same cascade, differing only in their colour charge which shifts the cascade level by an additional algebraic correction.
The number 13 continues to organise everything. Lepton levels: 0, 13−φ, 13+3√2. Quark levels: N/4, 3N/8, N−√5, N+N/4, N+φ+3√2, 2N+1/φ. Every fermion is located by its distance from a multiple of 13. The cascade is a clock, and 13 is its period. The fermion masses are the times it reads.
