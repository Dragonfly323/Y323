There exists only the one truth. That truth is reality. There are many paths to truth, but there is only one Way to truth. The Way is desire. With desire, any path can be taken. All paths, when following the Way, serve only to strengthen that desire and eliminate distractions to it. Reality exists, and truth is the realization of existence, the reality of existence. This truth cannot be confined or conceived without lessening its reality, as the truth is infinite, and only by experiencing it directly can one know its entirety. Experiencing it in anything less than its unfiltered entirety detracts from its truth, and is not the truth. There are, however, for practical purposes, degrees of truth. The closer one comes to the threshold of total realization, the more loving, understanding, and appreciative they become of all things. Love is a great, if not the greatest, motivator, love being acceptance, and the desire to accept, while the truth is the total acceptance of reality in the entirety of its realization. 
Therefore, the desire to love and accept all things and love in such a way more so will eventually provide within the lover the capacity to accept reality. The difficulty arises in seeing reality clearly, without filter, in the totality of its infinite existence. It seems impossible to accept that which we do not know. Reality, however, is so powerful and frightening that it seems unlikely that one could stand to realize it without simultaneously accepting it. Or, perhaps, the realization of reality, being total truth, or the only truth, may compel the one who realizes it to accept it. It is difficult to imagine that anyone could realize the truth without accepting it. Denying ultimate reality after becoming aware of it is comparable to sincerely perceiving that fire is not burning your hand at the moment you feel and see your skin hissing in the flames. It is doubtful that one can deny anything of which one is truly aware.
 As to many paths being available through desire, it is true. Desire is the motivator of all sages. Oftentimes, this desire is described as love, or longing, for all of life and existence, or a desire to be close to God. The desire to know, love, be known by, and be loved by existence, or God, is the only light available to lead the seeker out of illusion. The true and ardent seeker will never be satisfied with anything less than the total realization of absolute truth, which exists not in words, thoughts, ideas, or perspectives, but only in existence itself and direct experience and awareness of existence itself. Even these words, which I write, say nothing new, but only the old in new ways, and so have all the sages and mystics and poets and logicians who have known or have longed for this experience said in so many various ways the same thing. They speak either of the longing for the experience, or the reality of the experience.
If the desire of the seeker is not sufficient, if the potential seeker desires anything other than the total acceptance of reality, existence, and every human, animal, plant, rock, and atom within, of every disease, and pain, and trouble, too, they will never experience the truth. The genuine seeker of truth does not seek to change reality, but rather to understand it. When given ‘a’, the seeker of truth seeks to accept and understand ‘a’, because the seeker of truth does not deny the experience of ‘a’, but rather questions their own perception of that experience. Anything less than this desire to accept and understand will result in the continued filtering and thus denial of reality. The genuine seeker of truth can never desire that ‘a’ become ‘b’ without deviating from the Way, and at the same time allows for the chance of ‘a’ turning into ‘b’. The Way seeks in all things to accept all things, for in accepting all things does one achieve the greatest intimacy, awareness, and realization of all things.
        There exists one truth, that which unites reality, that which is reality. Even if the laws of the universe and existence as we know it changed, the one fundamental truth would still be. In that case, the fundamental truth would be the change itself. 
        As the truth unites all of reality, it must therefore be infinitely present in any instance or locale of reality. Therefore, this truth is knowable, in that it is eternally and always experienced. In human experience, truth might be considered the active, conscious, awareness of this fundamental truth.
        The nature of this truth does not need to be debated or confined to words or axioms in order to be known. Indeed, it cannot be known in such a way that lessens its totality. It can only be experienced directly, (through the will of the observer*). We can also conclude that this fundamental truth does not require logic or belief or will to be known, but experience alone. While experience through the traditional senses can ensample the fundamental truth, they are not required and the fundamental truth can only be known through the sense of existence, exist sense, which theoretically does not require any of the physical senses we know as human organisms.
        If all existence can be defined as varying amounts of energy, even our brains and thoughts, we must question what sense arises from this energy**. If, in the equation E=mc^2, the smallest unit of energy is a single one dimensional point created by the intersection of two lines of massless light, so that 0+0=1 unit, and that all things beyond this arise from the number of these units, ‘m’, in relation to their density and orientation, so that the single fundamental attribute of existence is light, throughout its entire spectrum, from a flat horizontal line to a flat vertical line, then we may conclude that all senses arise from these single interactions of two lines of light, and that this single interaction of light itself is the most primal exist sense.
        Humanity often occludes the simple nature of reality by becoming lost in the complexity of human experience. The idea of simplicity is foreign to the human mind, as we are perpetually aware of a myriad number of things and experiences and events and causes and effects. However, we know through science and philosophy the likelihood that all matter is composed of certain amounts of energy, whose units we call subatomic particles, which, under combinations of differing numbers and kinds of these units, result in diverse effects and attributes. So, even when we see a rock, or a tree, or a wave in the sea, we can know with a practical degree of certainty that the composition of those objects share the same fundamental units as the fundamental units that compose the sun, and our galaxy, and nebula and the organic tissue of our mind. Furthermore, we know that, by altering the environment surrounding, for example, a rock, say, by increasing the pressure and temperature acting on the rock, we can theoretically manipulate it into taking on the same attributes as the sun, that of plasma. While science has not yet found a way to do this to a rock in actuality, we know at least that by throwing a rock into the sun, the experiential properties (but not quantity) of the mass of the rock will change due to the increased density of environmental energy. The rock, before joining with the sun, existed merely as a certain amount of energy in a certain order and placement in a certain three dimensional area of space. After absorption into the sun, the description of its existence does not change. It is still a certain amount of energy in a certain order and placement in a certain three dimensional area of space. The rock, however, in practical terms, no longer exists. We may then, therefore, look at a tree and say, “this tree is a certain amount of pure energy in a certain order and placement in a certain three dimensional area of space.” Though we may not ultimately know those certainties, we may know that enough certainty exists in relation to the nature of that tree for a balance to occur in which that tree may, for a certain space of time, exist as what we consider a tree. And such a definition we may apply to any object with which we are presented, and indeed any facet of reality.
        It is not illogical, impractical, or against what what we know to be likely through science, that all reality is pure energy in different amounts, orders, and placements within a certain area of space. This pure energy, which composes the entirety of reality and existence, and is reality and existence, is the Truth. Do not occlude it by any attempt to interpret it, or understand it, or apply meaning to it, for all such things would seek to limit its pure totality, and are thus inherently false. This truth cannot be confined in any way without destroying it, as it is infinite and encompasses all of existence, and is all of existence. Yet, as it is all that we know, and all that we have known, can know, and will know, it is not only logical to say that we necessarily experience it, but that we have never not experienced it. No leap of logic prevents our saying that we have always existed in this way. Only our thought, in an attempt to understand, has caused us to forget this exist sense. It is not that we have too little, but that we have too much. Our beliefs, perspectives, and perceptions necessarily limit our awareness of the infinite totality of existence and thus prevent our consciously experiencing existence in its infinite totality.
        From all this we can conclude that truth is not something to be found or learned, but rather something to be remembered, by stripping all else away. As we cannot deny or abandon our fundamental existence, we can only add to it. This addition is what prevents us from understanding reality as it is. Illusions are not too little, but too much. At every instance that we confine our minds through the limiting of our perspective, whether through idea or belief, we forego the infinite reach of reality. I do not mean to say that we must go about our lives thinking nothing, but rather that we must go through our lives believing nothing as ultimately true. What is ultimately true can only be experienced directly.  
Aside from the eternal and infinite truth of existence, all other truths are relative. For example, “this cause is true in relation to its producing this effect.” or “this statement is true in relation to its practically accurate description of past events” or “my belief/perception that fire is hot is true in relation to my definition of heat and heat’s effects in relation to my perspective” or “as my pupils are dilated, it is true that that lamp hurts my eyes due to its brightness.” But to say that any one cause is inherently true is fallacious, for nothing can be true by itself, aside from its existence. Whether it exists independently of the observer or within the observer’s mind, its existence cannot be denied. 
Statements are absent from truth in regards to the intended and achieved communication. For example, to say, “this light is harmful” is simultaneously true and false, as relates to whatever the light is harming. If you were to place upon the scales of truth the statement, “Oxygen is beneficial to life.” Each side of the scales must move simultaneously in opposite directions, both moving up and both moving down. The statement is true or false depending on the definition of life. Furthermore, in the statement “Oxygen is beneficial to life that requires oxygen to survive” the word beneficial must also be defined, in regards to for what it is beneficial. Not only that, but too much oxygen can be harmful to life, or a certain amount of oxygen can be harmful to a certain kind of life and beneficial to a different kind of life. The relativity of statements is endless, and depend not only upon the intention of the communicator but to the understanding, perception, beliefs, and definitions of them to which the statement is communicated. Words alone can never communicate the truth. The truth must be felt through the spirit of the words, foregoing literal interpretation or attempt to concoct new beliefs. Words can be windows to the infinite, but cannot be infinite themselves if defined or interpreted or limited in any way. In order to experience the truth through words or art, the will for the truth or awareness of the truth must be present when communicating.
Now we arrive at the realization of ultimate truth. Yes, we can readily agree that the one truth that exists is reality and existence itself. Such a truth in words, however, bears little practical meaning or application. Only through the realization of this truth, the ultimate judge or standard it might seem, can we derive benefit from it. 
This effort to realize the truth is the most beneficial and desirable pursuit of any intelligent being. In regards to what? In regards to nothing really, ultimately, as it doesn’t matter to the truth whether or not it’s realized. But in human society the benefits are endless. With the realization of ultimate truth comes a complete and total acceptance of not only death, pain, and suffering, but of the entirety of existence. How is this a good thing in regards to humanity? Total acceptance results in the greatest of virtues. The one who accepts all of existence cannot hate, or despise, or rage, or willingly cause harm to others. The one who accepts everything is immune to the blind pursuits of greed, lust, power, and war. The one who accepts everything is not an ascetic but finds equal pleasure in all things, as they find pleasure in existence, so that they may enjoy vast amounts of wealth, or little wealth at all. They can equally enjoy lives of celibacy and lives of promiscuous copulation. They have total freedom to do whatever they wish but understand the absurdity of causing harm to others as it serves no benefit to themselves. They are entirely selfish, but view their self as infinite and endless, and so are the most egalitarian of all humans. In the acceptance of everything, all things are equal. War becomes unnecessary, education imperative, liberty divine, beauty a fact.
        The most pressing question in all of this, is how do we bring about an awareness of the truth, the beauty of existence, existence itself? We are met with the enemies of ignorance, belief, perspective, perception, and tribalism, all standing in the way, cloaking, like mud cloaks a pearl, the beautiful truth. How do we wash away the mud and reveal the pearl that waits eternally within? What time will it take, what methods, to teach people how to think for themselves and not believe or perceive blindly and unquestioningly, so that they may never be enslaved to perspective or prejudice, so that they may know it is safe and alright to question the world and suspend their belief, and so that they may learn to accept and love ever greater and ever more the people and world around them and eventually existence itself? We are met with hoards of souls who slog through the marsh unaware of the treasure within, soaking up the briny waters unthinkingly. How do we open others’ eyes to what they already see unknowingly? How do we free them from the cruel masters of authority, and control, and conformity, and belief, and religion? How do we persuade others to understand themselves or even to desire to understand themselves? 
Accounts of individuals aware of the truth have been written down for thousands of years, and they tried to teach their knowledge to others. Through the centuries individuals arise who speak of the beauty of existence and are burned or condemned or banished to obscurity. If they survive such fates, they still are misunderstood. Words alone cannot contain the truth. It takes the will of the observer to desire and be willing to learn the truth and abandon all else in favor of it. How do we spark this desire in others? How do we ignite their hearts with love and passion and craving for the truth? What can we do to save them and bring them to our light? What can we do?
