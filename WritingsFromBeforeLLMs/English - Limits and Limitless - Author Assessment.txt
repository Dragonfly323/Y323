I struggled a lot with the original versions of this piece. This time, I decided that since I couldn’t address the subject of the piece directly, I wouldn’t address it all. Instead, I detailed the undercurrent that led up to the event, the subject of the paper, and provided details of Ludwig Wittgenstein’s similar path and experience. With a longer paper, or perhaps a book, I could detail a few people who have had the same experience, but the experience itself is impossible to understand, so every person who has it finds great difficulty in expressing themselves to others. The experience defines you, but the experience cannot be defined. 
        So, I didn’t place stress on the incommunicability of the experience, and aside from some focus on symbols mentioned it only at the end. My first guess is that with this paper, the reader will have a curiosity about the subject, and wonder if questions will be answered. If everything comes together within the reader, the reader will be left with at least a vague understanding that it is absolutely absurd to think that truth is something that can be communicated. Then, perhaps, if they care, they will begin to look behind words and symbols, rather than at them, which leads only to confusion and falsity.
        There’s really nothing else in my life that even comes close in significance to this experience. I can write about it all I want, however inherently difficult it is to put into words, but all those words can ever be are symbols. And symbols, they don’t mean anything.
