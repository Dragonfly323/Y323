“I don’t think it is.”






                                                                                Dorothy whispered.


*
Danny walked ahead the others, his shoes marking the sand. The corridor was dark and musty, but the flames of the torches were burning, so they could breathe. It wasn’t time for the way to open yet but they were quite certain it was tonight. They had been there before, years ago. They were young and naïve, thinking adventures just happen. It was fortunate that Jimbone was such a fan of ancient mysticism or they would have missed it entirely. Now, they were ready for it.
The corridor opened into a small hollow, with a table in the center of a stone circle. In the table was a basin. In the basin there was nothing. The boys set their backpacks down in the corridor and the four of them formed around the center of the room.
“How do we know when it happens?” asked Davis.
“Shh” Jimbone shushed him. “We prepared everything beforehand, but what happens after isn’t clear. We just have to be here at the right time, and we are. No more talking. I don’t want anything to interfere.”
        Terry groaned under his breath. He thought Jimbone was a little too smart for his own good. However, if this did work out tonight, even he would have to give him a little credit.
        The boys waited. After a few minutes of standing quietly and checking to see if the moon were visible through the opening in the ceiling yet, Terry let out a sigh and turned away.
        “What are you doing, you idiot?” Danny hissed. “We need you! Get back in the circle!”
“Nothing’s happening.” Terry spread his arms impatiently. “Where’s the magic whirl?”
        “It’s going to happen.” Jimbone glared at Terry through his glasses and wisps of unruly red hair.
        “Fine, whatever, brat. I don’t care that I’m here because this temple is cool, but this is a waste of time.” Terry returned to the circle grudgingly.
