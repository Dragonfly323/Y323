“Did you know? Now they’re saying the universe began because something thought.” Lucy stood on the swing, swaying.
        “What? God? What do you mean, thought?” Miku slumped in the swing beside hers.
“Well, not God necessarily, but something. And the way I heard it, they mean ‘thought’ as in, like, some act of Will.” She said knowingly.
        “Well, yeah, that’s what the Bible says.” Miku said matter-of-factly.
“Oh, you don’t really believe all that yarn do you? That there’s some old man in the sky who only loves people, with all the horrible things they do?”
        “I don’t.. disbelieve it. And He loves everything, not just humans.” Then he added. “Well, if He were real, I mean.”
        “And why’s God gotta be a ‘He’, huh? Can’t God be a woman?” 
Miku started to feel uncomfortable. “Look, I don’t know, that’s just what people say, okay.”
        “Fat lot of good people like those are.” She clipped her hair up and got off the swing. “Are you coming for dinner?” She looked at him.
        “Yeah, if it’s alright.” He averted her gaze.
She rolled her eyes. “Can’t you just accept you’re welcome? It’s so annoying talking about it every time.” She pulled her hair back and squinted her eyes, mocking him. “Oh, Rucy, I courdn’t possibury impose on you and your famiry.”
        “Hey! Stop it!” Miku lept out of his swing to chase her. She ran away laughing toward her house.

“So, Miku.” Tabatha buttered her pancakes. “What have you and Lucy been up to?”
Lucy responded before Miku could. “We talked about the beginning of the universe. Not that you would know. You’re always living in that computer of yours.”
        “Hey, I’ll have you know I learn quite a lot about the universe with my computer.” She poured a tiny amount of syrup beside her pancakes. “Like, did you know, that there’s a rather big consensus among the scientific community that the universe began with a conscious thought?”
        “Yea-hus.” She said as if it were the most obvious fact in the world, insulted that her sister could accuse her of such ignorance.
        “Oh really?” Tabatha replied, stuffing a whole pancake in her mouth. “Am you fof faf I-”
“Yeah, why don’t you chew first, you slob.” Lucy shot.
        Tabatha took a gulp of almond milk and slammed the wooden cup down on the table like a sailor. “Don’t get cocky, Lucy. Remember I’m older than you.” 
“Yeah, but only by, like, four years!” Lucy had a bit of jam on her face.
“Four years is a big difference when you’re eight. You’ve only lived two-thirds of what I have.” She wiped the jam of her sister’s face, who allowed the action graciously.
        “You don’t know. I could be hundreds of years old!”
“But right now, you’re eight, okay?” She poured another glass of almond milk.
        Lucy slumped back and crossed her arms. “Humph. You’re the one who said I shouldn’t listen to appeals to authority.”
        Miku always felt a little out of the loop. He ate his pancakes silently, trying to understand what they were talking about.
Tabatha laughed. “Good, Lucy, that’s right. So what were- oh yeah, the universe. So you heard too? It’s all over the news isn’t it? Causing an uproar everywhere. The whole scientific community coming together like that, thousands of scientists, just to make that one declaration. I wonder what it means.”
“Well, maybe if you were hundreds of years old like me or Miku, you’d know it means that we can’t die.” Lucy talked as if she spoke of the weather.
Miku felt an opportunity to contribute. “We can’t die? Just because they say that thing about thought doesn’t mean anything. People still die.”
“Yeah, and then they go to Heaven, right?” Lucy said sharply.
“Lucy!” Tabatha glared at her.
        “It’s tru-uh!” Lucy pleaded. “He’ll never learn anything if he puts his head in a box like that!”
        Miku felt his face grow hot.
“Shush, Lucy. He can believe whatever he wants. It’s not hurting anyone.” Tabatha ate some more pancake. “Mfmu-” She sipped some almond milk, swallowed, and sighed with satisfaction. “Miku.”
“Y-yeah.” He tried to be stoic. 
“You don’t have to listen to Lucy. You just think whatever you want to, okay?”
        “Yeah, of course. I do. I mean. Well.. I like these pancakes.”
“Yeah, Miku.” Lucy joined. “It’s not like I care that much anyway.”
        Miku really did like eating pancakes for dinner.
Tabatha purveyed the situation. “Alright, we can talk about this later. The universe can wait.” She laughed and drank her third cup of almond milk.

        Miku walked home, troubled. They always troubled him. They were like aliens from some planet with intelligent life. Attempting to understand half of what they said left him exhausted. Lucy had a habit of bursting out like that. He didn’t mind. He didn’t know what to think about it either, though. No one was quite like Lucy, or Tabatha, for that matter. They were like adults. At least Tabatha was. Lucy puts him on the verge of crying. But no careless remark could blind him to the love she carried in her eyes. Ugh. Why does she have to be such a jerk, then. Still, Lucy was the smartest, kindest friend he had despite anything else.
