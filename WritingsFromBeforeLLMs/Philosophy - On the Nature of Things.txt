On the Nature of Things
An Intuitive Perspective on the Natural Laws




Introduction: Here I present the fundamental nature of the Universe. This empirical perspective emerges from applying mathematical, logic-based intuition on the known laws of the universe. The method I use relies largely on the observation of natural phenomena. For example, the interpretation of gravity and strong force as fundamentally attractive forces disagrees with an easily observed phenomena, inertia. This paper sets out to describe Gravity, Strong Force, Weak Force, and Electromagnetism all as various phenomena caused by the singular fundamental force of motion. At least, I hope to dispel the understanding of Gravity as an attractive force and detail how the idea of a fundamentally attractive force disagrees with all observable evidence.




1. The Foundation of Reality.
   1. Derivations of E=mc^2.
      1. One ‘c’ is a massless line.
      2. Two intersecting ‘c’s create a point of mass.
         1. Existence cannot contain a space with less than one point of mass.
         2. Existence has foundation of a 1c^2 fabric
      3. ‘m’ is the quantity of intersecting ‘c’s.
         1. Anything can be represented as a value of mc^2 within a definite volume.
   2. The Logic of Infinity.
      1. Spinoza’s Ethics.
         1. Two interacting substances must have a common attribute, an intermediary, through which they may act upon each other.
         2. As any part of existence can act upon any other part of existence, all aspects of existence must contain some common trait.
      2. Interaction of matter and energy.
         1. Commonly understood as through Four Fundamental Forces: Electromagnetism, Strong Force, Weak Force, and Gravity.
         2. In order for the four forces to act within the same reality, they must share a unified cause. 
      3. Concluding the existence of one principle, underlying phenomenon.
         1. The Four Fundamental Forces are manifestations of One Fundamental Force.
         2. Observational evidence and the absurdity of a fundamentally attractive force.

3. Laws of Motion and Fundamental Forces.
   1. Newton’s Laws of Motion.(1)
      1. Newton’s first law of motion
         1. Every body perseveres in its state of rest, or of uniform motion in a right line, unless it is compelled to change that state by forces impressed thereon.
         2. Derivations.
      2. Newton’s second law of motion
         1. The alteration of motion is ever proportional to the motive force impressed; and is made in the direction of the right line in which that force is impressed.
         2. Derivations.
      3. Newton’s third law of motion
         1. To every action there is always opposed an equal reaction: or the mutual actions of two bodies upon each other are always equal, and directed to contrary parts.
         2. Derivations.
   2. The Four Fundamental Forces.
      1. Electromagnetism
         1. The force that ordains the interaction of charged particles.
         2. Frequencies and spherical wave form.
         3. Lock and key schematic.
            1. The amount of energy in balance due to absorbed light determines the attribute/nature of exiting force/light.
            2. Possibly follows some sine/cosine waveforms, but uncertain as of writing. Difficulty conceptualizing in 3D. Provide 2D illustration.
      2. Strong Force
         1. The force that prevents subatomic particles from dissipating.
         2. The absorption of light, the repulsive force.
         3. Balance, imbalance, and scale.
            1. Heavily dependent on small size.
      3. Weak Force
         1. The force that causes radioactive emission.
         2. Unidirectional inertia overcoming opposing force.
         3. Balance, imbalance, and proportion. 
            1. Example: Continue adding force to a rotating ball on a string and eventually the force of the outward momentum will overcome the ability/force of the string to hold the ball.
      4. Gravity
         1. The Force that causes mass to display attraction of other mass.
         2. Absorption of omnidirectional incoming light by mass so that omnidirectional exiting force is always lesser.
         3. Difference in pressure, high density to low density, follows gas pressure laws and wave motion.
