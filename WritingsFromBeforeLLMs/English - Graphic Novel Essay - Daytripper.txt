Daytripper is a Brazilian graphic novel created by Fábio Moon and Gabriel Bá that centers on snapshots from the life of a Brazilian writer by the name of Brás de Oliva Domingos. Moving sporadically through various stages of his life, Daytripper creates a distinct story flow that results in a dream-like work of art. Daytripper addresses issues dealing with all the basics, love, life, death, childhood, old age but combines the core elements of them all through the use of Brás’ unique perspective as an obituary writer to abstractly show a take on the essence of existence. This Brazilian graphic novel is not a simple read and can be difficult to understand due to it’s disruptive storytelling and lack of a conventional ending that seems to be simultaneously inconclusive and too far reaching to be immediately satisfying. It’s obscurity, however, is it’s great strength, as it’s message cannot be conveyed directly but must usually rather be intuited through meditation on what has been read. The great achievement of Daytripper is that it is a realistic novel. It is a great achievement because the message the book carries concerning life and death is fantastic, and causes the perceptive reader to ponder the mythology of existence, yearning to know the breath of the wind as being profound. Daytripper gives the reader the awareness of the extraordinary within what is too often deemed the mundane and dull day to day life. From childhood memories to fateful plane rides to distant half-siblings, Daytripper reminds the reader that every event in life, no matter how quiet or little it may seem, is of the utmost importance and preciousness in the present moment, for one cannot know whether in the next moment he will die or continue to live. The art of Daytripper works mainly to the end of realism, as the message of the story and art are central to the concept of fantastic perceptions of day to day events and happenings.
