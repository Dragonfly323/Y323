The Peaceful River
A Goose’s Tale






Prologue


The peaceful river, flowing slowly in an early spring rain, was carrying a thing of great importance, something that would become the savior of the world, a goose. It was this goose, a teenager, as he was, who may have been the most ignorant in all the land of his own destiny. For he was only a goose, an ugly duckling, a peasant’s guard, a small particle of existence in all that was. But he was destined for more than simply being the third back on the left of the flying V. 
        Alphonse Recard Trinnaminy Errich was his name, though you may know him simply as “Goose”. For sake of writing, however, he will be referred to as Eric. Eric was born into a family of five new geese. Though he was born the second largest goose in the pod, through a series of rather unhappy misfortunes, he became the runt of his family, seeing that he was the only one left. Regardless, he complained little and stayed with the flock, never leading the V. 
        It was a good life, he thought. He had never heard of many other things outside the flock so he never felt like he was missing out. He had not once heard of anything beyond the rivers and marshes and swamps over which he flew. So he flew content in his own ignorance. But one day, perhaps it was the stars that did it, there awoke within him a slightly heavy feeling, right in his chest, where he had been told the heart of his flight was. The day this feeling awoke within is where our story begins. 


Chapter 1


        One late morning, as Eric was taking a bath in a river, he felt a strange pressure in his chest. This at first worried him for it was where he had been told his heart was. Why is my soul hurting? He thought. But as he felt the pressure a little more, he realized it was not quite pain. It felt as if his heart wanted to go somewhere. This seemed odd to him. Shouldn’t he know what it is that he wanted? Why would he have a longing for something of which he did not know? But still his heart ached. 
        He paddled slowly to the bank, for his heart was heavy and he feared that he might sink. He waddled up on the shore and turned to look at the river. He saw his flock bathing and, for the first time in his life, he thought, there must be something more. And with that thought he felt something indescribable inside. If he had known what a balloon was, he may have said it was something like having tied a balloon to all his doubts and worries and letting it go, watching as the balloon floated away. But that longing was still there. And he didn’t want it to go away. 
        With a spirit light as a feather, he gave one last look at his flock and flapped his wings and flew up to the sky. He hoped his flock wouldn’t follow him and that hope found root as he flew in silence. He didn’t know where he was going, but he trusted that whatever that feeling was inside him would guide him right. Unfortunately, after an hour or two, the feeling slowly began to fade until, at last, it was gone. The balloon that he had let go before came crashing back on him carrying more weights than it had carried away.
        It was getting dark, he was separated from his flock, and the only thing he saw was forest. Suddenly, he was cold and afraid; it was as if he were on some alien world.  His wings felt so heavy. He slowly descended to the unknown below, thinking perhaps that he find a tree that would hold him comfortably. He found such a tree and alighted on it. Now that he had stopped flying, the results of his actions were fully realized to him. He really was alone. 
        He had never been alone before. He was at first only at a slight unease. But with time, he began to grow dehabilitatingly frightened. At last, as he could take it no longer, he put his head under his wing and held it tight to himself until he fell asleep. 


Chapter 2


        He awoke with a start, and was cold. At first he did not know where he was, but gradually remembered what had happened. He was feeling much better by now and decided he would walk a little today. He flew down to the ground, conveniently onto a path. The dew wet his flippers as he took the sun for his bearings, thankful the clouds from last night had cleared, and set off on his way. 
        This forest was different from any forest in which he had ever been, though he could not tell why. The trees were trees, but they were different somehow. He wondered about this for quite the time. He was right in that this was a special forest, for it was civilized. This thought gradually made itself known to him in his mind. It was finally proven as he saw a fox walking toward him on the path.
        Generally, Eric would have turned tail and flew away but the visage of this fox was so striking that he stopped waddling. As the fox came closer, it seemed to glow brightly. 
        Soon, Eric realized that the fox was on fire, or made of it, he was not sure. The fox’s tail flicked near Eric’s face and he felt a burst of heat. No doubt this was a fox on fire. The fox stopped a little past Eric and turned its head to look at him. Their eyes met and Eric felt a pang of sickness. The fox looked away and continued on its journey. 
        Eric stared until he was forced to blink. But the moment he blinked, the fox vanished. Suddenly, Eric was filled with the most delicious excitement. He could feel the dew on his flippers and hear the wind blowing through the trees. The scent of the earth intermingled with the scent of fire and Eric drank in the sweet, heavy air. He remembered why he left. There really was something more. 
        With a renewed spirit of adventure, Eric began once more on the path. It was a good day for walking and he felt peaceful. At moments, he was convinced that the trees were watching him, but he did not mind, they seemed to him friends. The only animal he’d seen so far was the fox. 
        After walking for an hour or two, he saw a strange thing approaching him. At first he was scared but he put on a brave face and did not falter. As the animal came closer, Eric recognized it to, in truth, be yet another fox. But this one was different. It had on clothes and walked uprightly, with marvelously good posture. The fox and the goose stopped almost simultaneously a few feet from each other. 
        “Excuse me,” the fox said, “but have you seen another fox walking along this way today?” He sounded like a gentlemanly fox, not that Eric knew what a gentleman was. 
        “I’m sorry, did you say something?” answered Eric, after a brief and incredulous pause. Eric had only spoken to geese before, and indeed, from where he came, animals weren’t able to understand each other. So it was quite a strange thing for him to have heard words come from a fox. 
        With great patience and congeniality, the fox replied, “I asked if you had, by chance, seen another fox along this road today.”
        “What about it?” Eric said quickly, still in disbelief. “Relative of yours maybe?”
        “No,” the fox laughed, “I’m afraid not. Did you notice anything strange about this fox?”
        Eric was not certain if he should trust this fox. “Why are you looking for him?” he asked.
        Again with patience, the fox replied after a little hesitation, “I am a scholar doing research on such creatures, I have been searching for this kitsune for almost two months now.”
        “I am Eric,” the goose said. “What is research, Askollar?” Eric thought it was his name. 
        The fox looked for a second at Eric with a quizzical face before it disappeared and said, “Research is trying to learn more about something. And I fear you have misunderstood me. Scholar is my occupation, my job,” he added to be clear, ”and Henrick is my name.” 
        This embarrassed Eric slightly and made him feel a little stupid. He quickly shrugged this off and said, “Yes, quite, and I see no harm in that. Yes, I have seen such a fox. On fire even! It was the most beautiful thing.”
        “Oh really,” said Henrick, looking surprised, “on fire you say? Did it burn you?”
        Eric looked under this wing and that wing and turned around and wiggled his tail before saying, “I don’t appear to be.” 
        “Well that’s good then,” said the fox, “and I am glad I ran into you when I did. I’ve heard that the Kitsune is quite shy and appears rarely.” Here the fox put on some gloves, “And seeing as you’ve seen it this very day, there isn’t much point in looking for a while.” Now the fox was looking at Eric again, “So I’ll be off back to my home and I would be honored if you would accompany me there. Perhaps I can ask you more about the fox.”
        Eric immediately felt a sense of danger but he thought it was a gentlemanly fox, if he knew what a gentleman was, and having nowhere else to go, he decided to accompany the fox. “Alright then, I suppose we’ll be off now?” he said as he started off at a quick waddle down the path. 
        “I’m glad you’re coming,” the fox easily kept pace as he flicked his own tail back and forth, “I haven’t seen a single other soul today in this strange forest.”
        “And I’ve only seen two foxes,” said Eric as they walked beside each other, “What did you say your name was again?” And with that, the two continued through the forest like old friends, with Eric unaware that his real adventure had just begun.


Chapter 3


        “You must forgive me for the mess,” said Henrick, opening a small round door on the side of a hill, “I wasn’t expecting company while I was so close to the Barren Lands.”
        “Barren Lands?” said Eric questioningly. It was twilight and Eric was quite tired. They had talked of many things on the way over but not of the so-called “Barren Lands”.
        “Oh yes, I should probably tell you. You may want to sit down first.” Henrick offered a seat.
        Eric, who had been taught manners, remembered to thank Henrick before he hopped upon the stool and sat down as any goose would sit down, with his legs tucked under him. Thus being situated, Eric kindly said to the fox seated across from him, “Go on then.”
        Henrick gave a nod and began:
“I am curious about you, Eric. You seem to have no knowledge of anything around you. I’m not sure from where it is that you have come or how you came to find yourself here. But I encountered you deep in the Kitsune’s forest, which is the edge of the Barren Lands. I have been looking for the Kitsune for quite a while and I have not yet encountered a single other living animal other than myself in the two months I have been here. Yet there you are, from a direction as if you were coming out of the very heart of the Barren Lands, without a single speck of orange in your eye.
        “You see, one reason why it is called the Barren Lands is not that nothing lives there, as you can tell there are many trees, but because of a certain thing that happens to anyone who overstays their welcome. Their eyes begin to develop orange specks until their whole eyes are orange. Unfortunately, we do not know much of the cause or effects of this disease, or whatever it is. What we do know is that if you develop such orange specks to a certain extent, you will most likely find yourself unable to leave the Barren Lands or dead. 
        “I am risking my very life in my search for the Kitsune, but such is my passion. You, however, seem to have been there unwittingly. Not to even start with the fact that you saw the kitsune. Some believe that the Barren Lands drives you crazy, while others believe it gives you wisdom and knowledge and magical powers. It could be either or a mixture of two or nothing at all. Only those driven by a lust for power would knowingly enter deep into the barren land. And you hardly look like the power hungry type. So now that you have a vague understanding of the Barren Lands, I ask you, what were you doing there?”
        Stunned into silence by the many questions now pouring into his head, Eric sat still for a moment. Deciding he should just answer the first question for now, he said, “I’m not sure. I fell asleep in a tree after flying all day yesterday. I woke up and went down to the ground and began walking.” Henrick sat silently, which Eric took as a cue to say a bit more. “I didn’t think about where I was, I only knew that I was going somewhere. After I saw the kitten I-”
        “Kitsune,” Henrick said. 
“Kitsune. After I saw the kitsune, I continued walking until I saw you. And now I am here.” Eric finished. 
        Henrick, without a word, got up and went to a corner of the room, where he started a small fire and began to boil a small pot of water. He came back to his chair, sat down, picked up a journal, and began to write. After a few minutes of writing, he put the journal down. Shortly thereafter, the pot began to whistle. Upon hearing this, Henrick got up and went to the pot again. He poured water into two cups and put something in the both of them. Coming back, he said to Eric, “I don’t suppose you’ve ever had tea have you? It’s hot so be careful.”
        He set the tea down close to Eric, who had been curiously watching everything that Henrick had done, and returned at last to his chair. Where he situated himself comfortably.
        “Thank you,” said Eric, a little in wonder at the silence and topic change, “and no, I’ve never had tea before.” 
        “Most everyone likes it. I can get you some sugar if you’d like. Unless perhaps you find that you like it plain, as I do.” Henrick said. 
        “Sugar?” Eric said questioningly. 
For a while, Henrick had yet another amused look on his face before saying, “Triminny, you don’t know what sugar is? Where do you come from? Really?”
        Before Eric could answer, Henrick continued, reaching for a small covered bowl, “Here, try some, I’m sure you’ll like it.” Taking a small spoonful of white sand, he held it out to Eric who gently took the spoonful with his wings. After examining it, he chose to simply trust Henrick and tilted the sand into his mouth. 
        Eric did not know the word “sweet”. Thus, he had no way of describing the sensation and taste that he was experiencing. In fact, his first tasting of sugar was so powerful that his eyes blacked over and his brain paid attention for a short moment to only one of his senses, his sense of taste. As the only sense that was working was taste, it was as if he could see, hear, and smell the sugar and feel it all around him. He did not even notice when he dropped the spoon he had been holding. The few seconds that these feelings lasted seemed to Eric to be at least an hour. But as the last of the sugar melted away, Eric became conscious once more. 
        While Eric had experienced something incredible, all Henrick saw was an open mouthed goose dropping a spoon and drooling a bit. “I take it you liked it?” Henrick said, amused.
        Shaking his head to more accustom himself to reality, Eric said, “It was, it was,” Eric could not find the word to describe it. “It was good, uhm, no, it was different, different and good. Yes, different and good.” But still to Eric, the answer was not satisfactory.
        “Sugar is pure sweetness. As in, sugar tastes sweet. I can’t imagine what it must have been like tasting something sweet for the first time.” Henrick smiled. 
        “Sweet, yes.” Eric said to himself, remembering the word. Then, motioning towards the bowl, Eric asked “May I?”
        “Here,” Henrick said, “let me help you.” Henrick put two spoonfuls of sugar into Eric’s tea. Eric graciously accepted and tried the sweetened tea. 
        “Oh this is delicious,” said Eric.
“I’m glad you like it!” Exclaimed Henrick. A comfortable silence followed in which both Henrick and Eric sipped their tea and sat in the light of the one, lit candle. Before Eric was aware of it, he had fallen asleep. Luckily, he had finished his tea a bit before he slipped into dreams. 
        Eric awoke to a delightful smell that at the same time seemed both familiar yet altogether alien. Opening his eyes (he had fallen asleep right where he sat), he saw Henrick in a corner of the room cooking. 
        “What,” said Eric stretching his neck, “is that smell?” 
        Henrick turned around for a second and went back to cooking, “Ah, I see you are awake. I am making oatmeal, for the two of us. I think you’ll like it. It’s quite filling.” 
        “It smells like I’ll like it,” Eric said rustling his wings. “Good morning to you.” Eric remembered to say. 
        “Good morning,” said Henrick bringing two bowls of slop to the table. “Eat well,” he said as Eric tried the oatmeal. 
        “Thank you,” said Eric, “I’m not used to eating hot food. It’s quite good.”
        “Eating hot food is hardly necessary but it is a pleasure and it helps make us civilized.” Said Henrick, who was eating the oatmeal out of the bowl with his mouth. 
        “Civilized?” Asked Eric.
“Yes,” Henrick said, wiping some oatmeal off of his whiskers, “I am almost convinced that wherever it is from whence you have come is an uncivilized place.” Eric was giving Henrick the that tells me nothing look, so Henrick continued, “As you see, I am wearing clothes, and have been drinking tea and cooking oatmeal. I have chairs and books and writing tools. I am, for the most part, civilized.” 
        “Oh, I understand now,” said Henrick, speaking with a little difficulty as he was still eating, “am I civilized?” 
        Henrick laughed and said, “well you speak it and act it but you don’t dress it and you don’t know a lot about the civilized world. My guess is that you are a civilized goose from an uncivilized world.”
        “Alright then, I think I would like to be civilized,” Eric took a drink of water, “what do I need to do?”
        “You’ll only need some clothes and a little education.” Henrick smiled.
        “How am I to get clothes?” asked Eric.
“You’re pretty smart aren’t you,” Henrick said, “I would gladly buy you some clothes. But there aren’t any places to buy clothes around here. And here, you don’t really need them.”
        “So I just won’t worry about it now, is that it?” 
“I wouldn’t if I were you. Now I have something to ask you, Eric.”
        “Yes?” Eric said, paying attention.
“I told you I was looking for the Kitsune.”
        “Yes?”
“And you’ve seen the Kitsune.”
        “Go on.”
“I’d like you to help me to find the Kitsune again.”
        “How?” Asked Eric, slightly intrigued.
“I’m not sure if it will make a difference but as you have already seen it, I think it will more likely be seen by you again. And I want to be there when it appears, if it does.” Said Henrick.
        “So what am I to do?” asked Eric.
“The task sounds simple but it is actually somewhat dangerous.” Said Henrick. Eric felt a twinge of excitement.
        “What is the task?” asked Eric.
“You only need to accompany me into the forest again.”
        “Is that all? Where’s the danger? Not from you, is it? I think you’d have eaten me by now had you wanted.”
        “Yes, I could have,” said Henrick with a rather unsettling seriousness, “its dangerous because I don’t know what might happen in the Kitsune’s forest. The Kitsune is not the only thing that dwells therein.”
        The feeling in Eric’s heart that had lived in the beginning of his journey rose up again. “I’ll do it. I’ll help you.”
        “Then we can start today,” said Henrick slightly surprised at first but quickly forming a smile. 


Chapter 4


        Eric was waiting outside the house with his bag girt about him, thrilled at the prospect of an adventure, as Henrick shut the door and came to stand beside him. 
“Excuse me for making you wait,” said Henrick, “There were a few things I’d forgotten. Are you ready?”
        The excited goose at first did not comprehend what the fox had said but soon understood and said, “Yes, yes of course! Let’s be off!” and set off at a quick waddle down the path. Henrick the fox chuckled to himself after watching the goose hurry excitedly, almost tripping once over his webbed feet, as he went along the path.         But Henrick, being the kind fox he was, called out to Eric, “Wrong way!” before the goose had gone too far. Eric, undeterred, performed an about face and began walking at the same speed he had gone before in the exact opposite direction. 
        Henrick, still chuckling to himself, began following after Eric who began to circle him ecstatically (as he was moving too slowly for Eric’s liking) saying, “Come on! Get a move on it! You can’t expect to even reach the forest till by dark the speed you’re going at it. Come on, come on then!” After saying this, he waddled quickly in front of Henrick, then, seeing that Henrick was not picking up his pace, waddled just as quickly back, circling him again, giving more encouragements and motivations to walk more quickly. He then ran in front of him again. After having repeated this cycle a good three or four times, the goose realized that the fox would not be persuaded to adopt anymore swiftness than that of a gentlemanly fox on a leisurely stroll, as that is exactly what he was. 
        Henrick, at last, as Eric had given up and had, walking alongside him, adopted his companion’s leisurely strait as well, said “I’m glad I brought you with me, Eric, I think I’ll get quite hungry later on,” and pausing to see Eric’s reaction, which was only a slight glance, he continued, “No, in truth, I am glad I brought you, it is good to have company, and such humorous company at that, out here in this wilderness.” 
        “Do not mention it, Henrick. It is I who should thank you for taking me along. I do suppose if I had met anyone else, they really may have eaten me.” Eric said calmly, despite having been so excited before. 
        “I doubt you could even have found anyone else here.” Said Henrick, knowing for a fact that Eric was doing all he could to suppress his excitement. “Such a barren, lonesome place.” He finished with a cold look about his surroundings. 
        Eric, too, looked around at all the tall, beautiful, green trees, wildflowers, and speckles of sun falling through the living filter of leaves. It was unlike the forest in which he had been before, though he could feel they were drawing close. But in their current surroundings, he felt compelled to say, “It doesn’t seem all that bad.”
        “Perhaps you do not understand,” Henrick said, “why I say barren and lonesome, as it is seemingly covered with so much life. It is much the same way that people, surrounded by water, die of thirst. There is no life here.”
        “Surrounded by water,” Eric said as if repeating a riddle, “Yet dying of thirst.” Then looking earnestly to Henrick, he asked, “What does it mean? I’ve never heard such a thing, why, dying of thirst, while surrounded by water. How could such a horrible thing be true?”
        Henrick, eyes still looking straight ahead on the path, said, “It is called the sea, and perhaps you will one day go there.”
        “No thank you, kind sir,” Eric said adamantly, “If I will die of thirst while there! No sir, not the place for me.”
        “Oh such a case is rare and unlikely, especially if you’re still on land. You will not die if you go.”
        “I shall have to see for myself then, I suppose.”
“That is the usual way with these things.” Henrick stopped. 
        “What is it?” Said Eric.
“Quiet,” said Henrick, sounding slightly distressed. 
        Eric looked down the path but could see nothing that should cause any sort of worry; indeed, it was but an empty path. However, Eric stayed quiet, as he thought it best to listen to Henrick. They stayed quiet and still for what seemed to Eric to be a quite a while. At last, Henrick began walking his leisurely stroll again. 
        “Are you going to tell me what that was?” Eric asked.
        “What what was?” Henrick said slyly, glancing over to Eric, “I didn’t see anything. Did you?”
        “Wha- fla- it-” Eric stuttered at first but took a steadying breath and said, “Are you saying that we stood there for an hour looking at nothing?” Eric was a bit angry at first, but he could never stay angry for long and thus his anger gave way to befuddlement.
        “Is there anything wrong with standing still for a while and enjoying the forest air?” asked Henrick.
        Eric could only stop and stare, open beaked, at Henrick. 
        Henrick stopped and laughed, “Oh, the look on your face.” Henrick laughed again and said smiling, “You must always be cautious when entering the forest. I’ve been going in and out for years but I am always cautious.” Henrick began walking again. 
        Eric waddled quickly to catch up with Henrick, “Forest? We’ve been in the forest.” Eric said, understandably confused.
        “Ah, see, but not this forest.” Said Henrick, knowingly, “Look around you.”
        Eric had not noticed before the tall ancient trees that replaced the thick growth he could have sworn he was walking in the moment before and that he and Henrick were now walking on a path much larger and clearer than the one previously traveled. The entire forest had changed, without Eric realizing that even a branch was out of place.
        “Incredible,” Eric said in awe. He had not noticed such a change the previous night when he walked to the Fox’s home. 
        “Now you see but the surface of this forest’s wonders. It plays tricks on your mind, as you have just experienced.” Said Henrick. 
        “But not on you?” asked the goose.
“Ah,” the fox softly exclaimed, “it does. But after a while you learn to watch out for them.”
        “Like back then, when you stopped?” asked Eric.
“Aye. Very observant of you, Eric, I was indeed watching then.”
        “Were you watching for anything in particular?”
“There is nothing particular about this forest. And I do not understand enough of it to began telling you anything, as I am hardly sure myself what is and is not true.”
        “Ah, I see,” Eric was planning on asking Henrick to tell him more about the forest but it was as if Henrick read his mind. 
        The forest seemed heavy with silence as they walked on, joining their voices with the rest of the trees in a soundless lament. Such was this forest, a forest of sadness. Only at times, they found. Henrick was accustomed to the swings from sadness to elation but to Eric it was all of a newness. 
        “How are you fairing with this unpredictable weather?” Henrick asked, breaking the silence felt like taboo but Henrick knew better than to fall to the forest’s charms. And he knew that Eric did not. 
        “What?” Eric said, looking around, “it’s been clear all day.”
        “Think harder little goose.” Henrick was trying to teach Eric how to understand the forest, at least a little, as Henrick himself only knew a little, or so he said.
        And Eric thought harder. Soon a look of realization crossed Eric’s face, “You mean how I’m feeling at once sad, and then glad, and then adventurous and then bored?”
        “Very good, Eric, exactly.” Henrick said smiling, though then he felt sad, he had learned how to not listen to all the forest’s songs. Henrick continued, “Were I just a lost adventurer, I would surely lose my way soon if I were not already lost, in the mind I mean.”
        “What way in the mind do you mean?” asked Eric.
“I like to describe it as songs, the forest’s songs. It plays to you and gives you bittersweet sadness and excruciating joys. Of course it also plays melodies of pure sorrow as well as pure joy. Such experiences, I think, could cause one to wish to stay here and experience them all. Of course, I think it would lead one deeper into the Barren Lands, of which we are just on the brink. Ah, but this is all postulation, theory, so to say, I cannot know for sure. It could have a much less or much more frightening outcome.”
        “So much uncertainty,” said Eric.
“Hmm, yes.” Answered Henrick. They continued on through the storm until Henrick stopped and told Eric that it was time to leave. They had gone far enough and could not be expected to make it back on time if they went farther. 
        Henrick had never spent the night inside the Kitsune’s forest and had no desire to change that fact. Eric was in concordance and after having a small, but filling lunch of oatmeal, they walked the afternoon away back the way they came. They reached home in fading dusk. 
        “That’s strange, I don’t remember leaving the door open.” Henrick said as he saw the hole in the side of the hill. 
        “Oh, then do you remember,” Eric asked uneasily, motioning toward the nearby bushes at what was left of the circular wooden door, “violently tearing it off?”


Chapter 5


        Henrick lit a candle and sank to his knees, as the aftermath of whatever chaos had invited itself into his house overwhelmed him. For a moment, he thought himself still in the Barren Lands, in the Kitsune’s forest, and that this was all a horrible dream. For a moment, he believed, if just for a second, that two years of his hard work had, in fact, not been destroyed, ransacked, pillaged, or burglarized. But the moment was fleeting, and the reality of what happened came crashing down on him. He had been robbed, he was unsure of the extent but he knew already what this meant. 
        Eric stood silently at the doorway, unsure what to do as his friend kneeled in what was left of his home. Henrick put his face into his hands, took a deep breath, and stood up. He wiped the dust from his pants and turned to face Eric. 
        “Sorry everything is such a mess.” He said, smiling with his eyes closed. “But no use trying to clean this up right now.” He laughed. 
        Eric walked in, “What happened?”
“Ah,” Henrick sighed, righting his favorite chair, which had been gutted of its stuffing, “I seem to have been robbed.” Henrick took a quick look around, “Or maybe,” he looked at Eric, “Nothing was taken.”
        “I don’t understand,” said Eric, “Why would they rob you and take nothing?”
        “Because,” Henrick said, rising and picking up the remains of a once valuable vase, “They didn’t find what they were looking for.” Eric sat thinking over Henrick’s enigmatic answer as Henrick returned to his chair. 
        “What were they looking for? How can you be so sure? Could it not have been a simply robbery?” Eric asked, hating himself knowing that he wanted, deep down, for it to be more than a simple robbery.
        “I can be fairly sure.” Henrick said. Then, pulling out from his pocket the journal in which Eric had often seen him writing, he spoke a single word more, “this”, in reference to the journal.
        Eric and the fox sat in silence in a mutual fascination of the leather bound book for but a brief moment, only long enough for Eric to get confused, before Henrick continued, “Had they taken things I may have deemed it a simple robbery but given that everything was broken and we are in such a remote location, I can only surmise that they were looking for something in particular, which they didn’t find as it was never there in the first place.”
        “You mean the journal?” said Eric. 
Being in no mood for sarcasm, Henrick replied plainly, “Yes, the journal. Fortunately, I always carry it with me. We are lucky we were not here at the time of the crime as I am sure we would be just as broken as everything else here.”
        “Well what is in the journal? Do you delight in tormenting my curiosity?” Eric questioned with a sense of stress about his words. 
        Glancing away from the journal, Henrick gave a sly smile, “I am sorry friend; old habits are hard to break. Forgive me.” He, turning himself to fully face Eric, continued, “I will tell you the short version and I may leave a few details out as I see fit and when they slip my mind. The Kitsune, while my main subject, is not my only research. Only, while my research upon the Kitsune is surely questionable, my other research is downright forbidden. For some reason the Empire has –“ 
        “Empire?” Eric interrupted.
“The people in power than,” Henrick continued, “have outlawed any research on the Barren Lands. Research on the kitsune is merely an excuse to be here, barely allowed and only as I am backed by a prestigious university. Whoever came here came for one of two reasons: the first, they were looking for a reason to execute, I mean kill, me, I have more than a few enemies, and the second is that they researched the Barren Lands as I do and meant to take my notes from me. Henrick chimed in, “The Barren Lands are dangerous, deadly. It is the role of the government to prevent curious or adventurous people from endangering themselves or the populace. They are a place of danger, death, disease, and darkness. At least, officially.”

“Let’s sit down shall we?” offered Henrick and the three sat down in each their own way.
        “There is something in the Barren Lands.” Said Henrick, his tail flicking with stifled excitement.
        “Something, for sure.” added Ricky the Badger knowingly.
        “Yeah?” Said Eric in tensed wonder. “Yes? What is it?”
“Nobody,” Said Henrick slowly, leaning forward, with an excitedly crazed look in his eyes, “knows.” Ricky chuckled softly. Henrick stood up, stretched his arms and leaned neatly against a wooden supporting beam.

“What?” said Eric in disbelief, “This is too much, can you stop going all around whatever it is you’re saying and just say it? Please? I can’t take it anymore!”

“Looks so.” Replied Ricky with an equivalent grooviness.
        “You’re curious aren’t you?” Said Henrick, turning back to Eric.
        “Yeah, a bit.” Said Eric with an irritated sarcasm.
“Well so are we, boy.” Said a relaxed Ricky.
        “Just as curious as you.” Henrick whispered intently.
“I don’t understand.” Said Eric.

The smoke from the Autumn fires delighted even the youngest of the city’s inhabitants and calmed even the most anxious of souls. The Summer had long ended, but Eric just now began to feel the cold, as if it were his own feathers, and not the leaves, which lost their hold upon their root. And Eric began to feel afraid; as a horse might lower his mane at the deceitful breezes of a darkened cloud, so did Eric’s soul bow down before an unseen force, a terrible fury, an approaching storm.


Chapter 10


        In the morning, with the dint of a glowering sun peeking through the dusted window, Eric was awoken by the smell of cinnamon and oats. Geese like cinnamon, it is little known, and Eric was, even for all that he was not, a goose. 
        “Hey!” he said suddenly, his eyes snapping open, “heavens, is that cinnamon?”

“Heaven.” Ricky said, nonchalantly, “Heaven. It’s..” He trailed off. “It’s no matter, really.”

Have to consider the cost of the goods, who’ll be buying, whether the tailor shop in the Westerly Quarter is doing any good this year, whether or not they be buying more or less than I, how much they’ll be charging, and, most important of all, how cold it will be this winter.”

“Eric,” said Ricky, “You’ve never stayed here for winter have you, my boy?”

He turned around with a deliberate emotion, and shut the door, as if he were acting in a play, before turning around once more in a deliberate motion to face Ricky. “There!” he said, with a different inflection than the first, though the excitement remained.

“A bag of meat.” Henrick said, with a suddenly dead face.

Ricky hissed, “What?!” as he paced quickly to the peek out the window and loose the curtains, bathing the room in a new dusk. “What- Henrick, you can’t do this. You can talk to me about the Barren Lands, sure that’s one thing, but bringing” his voice dropped to a whisper “meat? Are you mad? Are you ignorant? Are you crazy or a total fool?”

        Henrick rose slowly, brushing of his pants, “I have only shown you the state of us all.”
        “Bloody hell, Henrick,” Ricky slumped down in a chair, burying his face in his massive paws, “you’ve ruined me. I was happy. Henrick went to him and gave him an embrace that he returned to Henrick.
        “It’s over.” Henrick said.


Chapter 11
