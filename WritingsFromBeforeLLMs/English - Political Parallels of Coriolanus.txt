


While Coriolanus offers many points of discussion, what most stood out for me was the depiction of the political process in relation to tribalism. Multiple times in Coriolanus, the deciding factor of election, the public, are swayed by words and appeals that lack any firm foundation. Brutus and Sicinius, the tribunes, manipulate the public through tribalism. They tell the proletariat that Martius will treat them cruelly because Martius is from another social class and cannot possibly understand the pains and concerns of the common people. They say to the proletariat that as they are from their class and are their representatives, they alone know and will do what is best for them. Martius is pragmatic. He is not politically minded and cares little for the concerns of the proletariat. In this, the tribunes are right. However, while Martius appears callous, he also appears noble and honourable and the proletariat recognize this when they see him. As soon as he is gone, though, Brutus and Sicinius turn the public against him again. 
The motives of Brutus and Sicinius and Martius and the rest of the characters are not as important to me as the reaction of the public to the tribune’s appeal to tribalism, an idea of us-versus-them. The public latches onto this idea of being separate and divided and turn into a vicious mob intent on securing the benefit of their own group. Tribalism, this primal, basic instinct of humankind, is arguably humanity’s greatest weakness in the civilized age. Any leader needs only create an idea of division, of us-versus-them, and a sense of danger, of imminent threat, and the public is held under sway. There is no logic or sense in it. A leader can say to a group of people that a great danger lurks outside and that only by banding together against the enemy can they survive. Those people will fall under a collective mentality. This tendency is detrimental to the public well-being and the well-being of the world. If a group, a culture, a religion, is chastised for an unhealthy practice, the leader needs only to say that the chastisers are attacking the group, that they are attacking what unites them. What hate group has ever existed that did not have a dogma of an outside enemy, a threat? There is the Jewish threat, the Nazis might say, or the black threat for the KKK, the white man for the Black Lives Matter protesters. These overgeneralizations serve only to harm. In everyday politics, there are the Republicans and the Democrats. Each blames the other for all the problems they face. This in particular is a great distraction.
While wars, the drug prohibition, the prison industrial-complex, poor education, and all other ails continue under both banners, the public who ally themselves with one side say all ailments of the country are the fault of the other side and forego objective, logical analysis. The real issues are complex, but they continue under both parties in the USA, much through the use of news networks. The media puts out a story of police brutality, leading to massive protests and riots and a countermovement, all of which support a feeling of division. Police brutality has an insignificant impact on society, compared to other ails. We are told to focus on the division between whites and blacks, that the condition of blacks in the USA are a result of this violence of one group upon another. The media of today are the Brutus and Sicinius of Coriolanus. The media inflates issues that have little societal impact. Police brutality and shootings are horrible, of course. It happens. Sometimes it’s an accident; sometimes it’s not. The societal impact is little, however. The media supports a narrative of us-versus-them, blacks-versus-police, deteriorating relations and distracting from the real issues, just as Brutus and Sicinius turn the proletariat against Martius and distract from the famine and their own lack of power to help the people. The people of the USA are divided on superficial issues. The politicians speak about Black Lives Matter and immigration and political correctness and gender equality and police brutality and how we should be accepting or defiant but what do these things do to change society? I do not say they do not change society, but that they change society little compared to more central issues that both parties support and dare not mention. By keeping the people divided on issues that do not affect the undercurrents of the societal structure, politicians are able to continue their various and corrupt dealings in the business world. Who makes or loses money whether homosexuals can get married or not? Who makes or loses money whether police are brutal or not? Who makes or loses money depending on what terms we use when talking to people or about them? We are kept divided on these issues so that no time or attention can be given, no peace can be had, to allow thought of those things of greatest effect, those things that do affect whether someone makes money or, more significantly, loses it. By appealing to our primal instinct of tribalism, politicians can move us like puppets, distracted and thinking falsely that our actions have impact, while they do whatsoever they wish backstage. Coriolanus gives fine example of just how easily every person is subject to this emotional appeal.
