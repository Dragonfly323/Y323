


Limits


Language is deficient. It always has been and always will be. When we write, we try to capture a multifaceted, expansive expression with a single thread. You can only read one word at a time. By the time you are finished reading, can you see the whole? Have you forgotten what went before? When the thread is followed to its end, often all that’s left is a tangled heap of words, an unwoven garment.
        We can only communicate through words that which we already know, at least in some measure; we must have basis to build what we wish to convey. How could you tell a Saharan dweller about the vast forests of California? If they have seen a tree, you could try explaining how that tree could be many trees and taller and stretch as far as one can see. But what if the Saharan dweller has never seen a tree? How could they ever see the forest? Could they ever understand? Alas, you cannot communicate an unshared experience.
        What is a human if not a culmination of such experiences? What friendship is ever founded upon uncommon ground? We bond over what we share. What we do not share, however, we can never fully understand of each other. Fortunately, we share much and we can understand partially most things. I have not been to Finland, though I have seen pictures and videos and read of the country’s culture and history. I can never understand fully that experience of my feet upon Finnish ground, but I can use what building blocks I have to imagine it, however partially. 
What most greatly separates us are those unique experiences which rebel against words and language and speech, those moments when we feel or see in silence what our minds refuse to confine or understand in words. Though not necessarily unique, they are entirely individual things impossible to share. You can paint your words around them, make a frame for that art you cannot speak, but unless the other has experienced the same to some degree, has seen a glimpse of what you have seen, they can never understand your words. You are speaking a different language.
        I have said all this so that you might understand that I do not expect to be understood. The single culminating event of my life is one of those unspeakable moments. I say “is” rather than “was” because it continues today, though it first began, or occurred, one day in August of 2015, when I was driving back from my brother’s in Raleigh to my home in Greenville, NC. To say simply, my perspective changed. I drove with the endless highway stretched out before me, the pine forests parted like the Sea, the sun among the clouds ahead. Then, suddenly, though nothing changed, everything changed. I saw everything differently. In an instant, something in my mind clicked. It has been the same since.
        I can’t describe it in any way beyond that, no one can. If it’s clicked for you, you’d know immediately what it was. We would share that experience and speak the same language. I didn’t want to write about this, because it is so nonsensical and I know that very well. Yet there is nothing comparable to its significance in my life. I can’t say how it illustrates an aspect of myself. It entirely refuses to be put into words, as if it were a living thing, arms outstretched against the door frame; it can’t fit inside. It laughs at you for trying. So I am resigned to dance around it, and paint frames and make asides, never speaking of it directly. I could say it is me, who I am, but that sounds the most false of all.
        You must understand that this event was not some blind moment of sudden epiphany. I knew about it to some degree beforehand. I knew I couldn’t understand it until I experienced it. I saw it off in the distance. I tasted it long ago.
        I was eleven years old when it first occurred to me that there was something out there. It felt like it was on the tip of my tongue, or the tip of my mind. I searched for it for eight years. I made serious inquiry into it. The more I learned about it, the more I studied the frames of others’ words, the pictures they painted of this thing they could not say, a thing I recognized from the taste, the more I resolved to quantify it.
        I began to consider myself a logician, and have become ultimately so today. I was not so devoted before, but realized at some point that I needed logic to paint my own frame, even though I didn’t know what picture it held. I moved slowly as I could, making note of every shift in my perspective, hoping that I could mark some path to it, some definite, communicable way. I wanted to be able to say, “Do A, then B, then C and you’ll arrive at your destination.” I kept track of all my letters to this end. But in the end, all I had was alphabet soup. To this day, I don’t know how it happened or why. I don’t think I ever shall.
        I expect you might be a little disappointed. I promised you a garment, an unwoven garment at the least, but I have given you the Emperor’s New Clothes. I expect these words say nothing at all, that the thread rests invisible in the empty phrase. I have said nothing at all of what I wished to convey. I’ve told you when it happened, and where, and what led up to it, but nothing of the thing itself. I have shown you the limits of language. All that remains are the crumbled stones of a broken tower. The construction is failed, the garment ruined; these words say nothing of time or place or whether silence is the better answer. We are left in the dark, I along with you, but I have seen the silence. 


“Perhaps this book will be understood only by someone who has himself already had the thoughts that are expressed in it–or at least similar thoughts. The whole sense of the book might be summed up in the following words: what can be said at all can be said clearly, and what we cannot talk about we must pass over in silence. Thus the aim of the book is to draw a limit to thought, or rather–not to thought, but to the expression of thoughts. It will therefore only be in language that the limit can be drawn, and what lies on the other side of the limit will simply be nonsense.”
-Ludwig Wittgenstein
From Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus, 1921
