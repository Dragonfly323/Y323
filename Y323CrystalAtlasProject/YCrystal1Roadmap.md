## 0. Core Vision

The Mathematical Crystal Atlas is an open-ended organizational framework for exploring a mathematical structure through multiple simultaneous languages, domains, representations, symmetries, dynamics, and applications.

Central object: $\mathcal{C} \in \mathrm{Mat}_7(\mathbb{K})$, together with:
$$\mathcal{Z} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{C} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{Y}_{64} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{S} \leftrightarrow \mathcal{P}$$
where $\mathcal{Z}$ = axial-zero structures; $\mathcal{Y}_{64} = \{Y^i_{jk}\}$ = the 64-fold transformation family; $\mathcal{S}$ = symmetry descriptions; $\mathcal{P}$ = physical/geometric/informational/computational projections.

**Organizing principle:**
$$\text{small axiomatic core} \rightarrow \text{large structure graph} \rightarrow \text{many representations} \rightarrow \text{many domains} \rightarrow \text{discovery}$$

The crystal stays compact. The Atlas of its relationships becomes enormous.

## 1. The Atlas Is Not a Single Theory

The Atlas accommodates three classes of discovery:

- **Recognition** — $A \cong B$ (equivalence)
- **Translation** — $A \xrightarrow{\Phi} B$ (useful representation without equivalence)
- **Emergence** — $A \otimes B \rightarrow C$ (new structure from interaction)

Type III (Emergence) is especially important: the Atlas must record mathematics *generated* by interactions, not merely present in any individual region.

## 3. Seven Simultaneous Objectives

1. Recover the exact mathematical object
2. Formalize the object and its structures
3. Discover internal symmetries
4. Construct translations between mathematical languages
5. Explore dynamical realizations
6. Map relationships to existing disciplines
7. Extract computational and technological structures

These are simultaneous, not sequential. Progress in one reveals new questions in others.

## 4. Key Principles

**Many Languages:** Every important object should be tested across representations — at minimum: matrix, operator, geometric, dynamical, informational. When independent representations reveal the same invariant, record an *Atlas Convergence Event*.

**Symmetry Observatory:** No symmetry family is predetermined. Scan for $\mathbb{Z}_4, \mathbb{Z}_7, \mathbb{Z}_2, S^1, SU(4), G_2, \mathrm{Spin}(7), SO(7)$ and whatever emerges. Symmetry is an observatory, not a destination.

**Domain Principle:** Applications are additional mathematical viewpoints, not terminal destinations. Template:
$$\text{Crystal representation} \rightarrow \text{domain variables} \rightarrow \text{equations} \rightarrow \text{observables} \rightarrow \text{simulation}$$

**Unknown as First-Class:** Unknowns, conjectures, anomalies, and unexplained correspondences are valid Atlas entities. A conjecture may remain a conjecture.

**Preserve Before Compress:** $\text{preserve} \rightarrow \text{index} \rightarrow \text{organize} \rightarrow \text{formalize} \rightarrow \text{compress}$. Compression is only safe after structure is understood.

**Open Taxonomy:** Atlas structure $=$ evolving taxonomy constrained by accumulated structure. New categories may be added, split, merged, generalized, or retired. The Atlas must be capable of changing its own map.

## 5. Progress Is Multidimensional

Each branch carries a state vector $(F, P, K, C, X, G, Q)$:

- $F$ = formalization; $P$ = proof depth; $K$ = structural coherence
- $C$ = computational realization; $X$ = cross-domain correspondence
- $G$ = generativity; $Q$ = compression

Summary scalar $\mathfrak{A} = (F+P+K+C+X+G+Q)/7$ may be displayed but never replaces the vector.

Progress gates (illustrative, not immutable): $F \geq 8, C \geq 7$ for initial level; $K \geq 8, X \geq 7$ for symmetry; $X \geq 9, Q \geq 8$ for Atlas gate.

Three forms of progress: **formal** (object more precisely defined), **structural** (relationships understood), **generative** (structure produces new mathematics). A mature Atlas should generate conjectures, invariants, algorithms, representations, and new objects — not merely catalog existing ones.

## 6. Formalization and Computation

**Formalization** is a parallel layer, not a prerequisite. Proof tiers: Tier A (definitions) → B (algebraic identities) → C (structural) → D (representation) → E (dynamical) → F (cross-domain) → G (deep structural). The system simultaneously contains exploratory, computational, conjectural, and mechanically verified mathematics.

**Computation** is an investigative instrument. Eventual capabilities: symbolic manipulation, spectral analysis, orbit/stabilizer computation, simulation, visualization, automated comparison, conjecture generation. The Crystal Explorer vision: select an eigenmode, inspect its spectral value, associated $Y^i_{jk}$, $(\zeta,\theta)$ coordinates, symmetry relationships, geometric interpretation, dynamics, and formal theorem references.

## 7. Collaboration and Navigation

The Atlas supports heterogeneous investigators: human researchers, AI systems, theorem provers, simulation agents. Different investigators use different languages; the Atlas provides the common structural layer. $\text{many minds} \rightarrow \text{one evolving map}$.

Navigation is nonlinear. Example traversals:
- $\mathcal{C}_7 \rightarrow \mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{C}) \rightarrow (\zeta,\theta) \rightarrow \text{dynamics}$
- $\mathcal{C}_7 \rightarrow \mathcal{Y}_{64} \rightarrow \text{symmetry}$
- $\mathcal{C}_7 \rightarrow \psi \rightarrow (M,g) \rightarrow R_{\mu\nu}$
- $\mathcal{C}_7 \rightarrow \Sigma \rightarrow I,J \rightarrow \mathcal{T}(\lambda)$

No traversal is privileged.
