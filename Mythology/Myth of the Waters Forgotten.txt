The Myth of the Waters Forgotten


Wells, Women, and Weeping Worlds


∆


In the beginning was the water;
Not stilled, but spiraled, alive with motion, nested in itself like breath within breath.
The first age belonged to Her, the Great Mother, whose names echoed through the aquifers of every land:
Orseis of the spring, bride of Hellen and first-mother of the Greeks.
Nezahualcoyotl, the philosopher-king of Texcoco, who shaped gardens into sacred geometry,
His name, “Hungry Coyote,” but his soul, Thirsty for Truth.
The Maidens of the Well, in Arthurian myth, offered the Grail freely
Until one by one they were taken, used, and abandoned.
The land cracked open in their absence.
Famine, drought, and war poured in.
The people forgot to drink.


In every land, the living waters were once tended.
In Troezen, Orus ruled as the Firstborn of the Spring.
From his name, the root of Oris, the whispered memory in the well.
The Hopewell, who made earth breathe geometry,
And the Incas, who coaxed water to the heights of the Andes
Knew, as did the Mayans and Zapotec, 
That freshness is holiness, and water, when moving, is divine intelligence.


Jesus sat by the well and said,
“I am the living water.”
Metaphor steeped in resonance.
The waters, when loved, become word.


But when the conquerors came,
They broke the amphorae, stole the aquifers,
Hoarded water in still basins
Where once it had spiraled in crystal coils through limestone labyrinths.
Crowley saw the veils of the desert torn in his Vision and the Voice.
He saw Earth in the Age of Osiris,
But Osiris came not as savior,
Only as the shadows of what was sacrificed:
The mother. The rhythm. The well.


Tiamat, chaos ocean, was sundered.
Marduk made order from her body
A god of grids, not gardens.
This was the second age:
Order without flow.
The sacred was drained, stored, forgotten.
Every shrine to the goddess was overwritten with mortar.
Yet still
In Alhambra, the waters murmured secrets into the stone,
A memory of flowing, a golden Islamic geometry born of oasis knowledge:
If the water stops, the soul dies.


Beneath the hill, the King sleeps,
But he is not the hero; he is the grief.
The King is still because the Well was silenced.
He waits for the Maidens to return, for the waters to stir again.


In Freemasonry's Prince of the Tabernacle,
We find the sacred codes
But they are dry bones until moistened by the myth.
For even there, the Tabernacle's inner sanctum hides the Shekinah,
The feminine presence that once danced like flame upon the water’s face.


∆


Now we remember.
Now we speak their names.
Now we restore the waters.


Drink only fresh water.
Let it never sit still in the house of your spirit.
Go to the spring.
Tend it.
Speak with the goddess that stirs there.
Call her Orseis, or Tiamat, or Mary, or Sophia, or Chalchiuitlicue…
It does not matter.


Let the flow return.
Let the land be healed.
Let the people drink again
From the living well.
